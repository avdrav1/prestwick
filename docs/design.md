# Prestwick: design summary for implementation

Source of intent: the Claude Docs design doc "Prestwick, Game Design Doc". This file holds the numbers and rules code depends on.

## Grid and terrain

- Tile = 5 yards. Isometric 2:1, 64x32 footprint. Height 0 to 7 steps, 16 px riser per step.
- Grid size (D4): 5 yards per tile everywhere, greens included. A 30 yd green is about 6x6 tiles.
- Height model (D5): heights are whole steps, drawn as terraces with risers. There are no ramp tiles and no sub-step heights. A "ramp" is the slope the ball feels between two neighbouring tile centres one step apart: 2 yd over 5 yd, 40% (21.8 degrees), from the interpolated height in `golf/height_field.gd` (see Ball). E1-S10 draws an edge cue on rises that face away from the camera, which otherwise show no riser and look flat (next line). On green the ball feels a step as a 3% break, not 40% (`slope_scale`, see Ball; E1-S11), and a bank of two or more steps per tile as steep (`steep_scale`, E2-S4).
- Edge cue (E1-S10, `art/specs/edge_cue.md`): a tile higher than its back neighbour (x - 1 or y - 1) draws a lip line along that edge, a dark line outside the top and a bright one inside. One lip is a rise of one step, two lips are two or more, so the count reads in grayscale. Level or higher back neighbours and the map border get none. It draws over its tile's top and under objects on it. No tunables: the step classes are art frames.
- Projection (`course/iso.gd`, the only one): tile (i, j) at height h has its center at screen `x = (i - j) * 32`, `y = (i + j) * 16 - h * 16`. Draw order is back to front by i + j, then risers bottom up, then the top.
- Camera: zoom steps 0.5x, 1x, 2x; WASD pan 600 screen px/s at every zoom; middle-mouse drag; center may go 128 px past the course edge. Tuned in `scenes/camera_tuning.tres`.
- Play camera (E2-S8, `scenes/course_camera.gd`): in Play the camera centres on the ball when Play starts (on the tee), when a shot starts, while the ball moves, and after it rests or is dropped. It eases onto the ball with a time constant of `centre_time` 0.15 s in `scenes/camera_tuning.tres` (95% of the way in 0.45 s; 0 jumps), so a moving ball stays centred without a jolt at each stroke. A pan (WASD or middle drag) stops the centring so the player can scout; `snap_camera` (C) snaps back to the ball and centres on it again. Zoom does not stop it. A shot starting centres on the ball even after a pan. While the player aims at rest (E2-S8 review, pillar 2), the camera frames the ball and the aim marker together: it centres on their midpoint, held so the ball stays at least `aim_frame_margin` 64 screen px inside the edge, and when both do not fit it steps the zoom out (never past 0.5x) until they do. On `courses/example_par3.json` the driver's marker from the tee is (1568, 784) px away at 1x, so the tee opens at 0.5x; the 6-iron's (1005, 502) fits at 1x. A flight follows the ball alone at the zoom the aim chose, and the next aim (Play or the ball at rest) starts again from the player's own zoom, the level of their last zoom key. A zoom key wins over the fit until the next aim, and a panned camera never zooms. The swing panel hint names WASD (pan) and C (back to ball).
- Brush sizes 1, 3, 5: an n x n square centered on the cursor tile, clipped to the map. Sizes and the undo depth (200 steps) are tuned in `course/builder_tuning.tres`.
- Plot (D10): a new sandbox (E2-S3) is 128 x 128 tiles (640 yd along an edge, 905 yd corner to corner, about 85 acres), every tile rough at height 2. A par 5 needs more than 94 tiles of straight line (470 / 5), so a 520 yd par 5 (104 tiles) fits along an edge with 24 tiles to spare, with room for water on its line. Height 2 lets the builder dig two steps for water and bunkers and raise five. The plot's cost is E4-S1's. The main scene started on this plot from E2-S8 to E6-S5; from E6-S6 it starts on a New course (Land). New sandbox (a Build HUD button, `CourseBuilder.new_sandbox`) makes it from `plot_size`, `plot_surface` and `plot_ground` in `course/builder_tuning.tres` and hands it to a fresh builder (empty undo stack). Measured at 128 x 128 (E2-S3, RTX 3080, Godot 4.7.2 debug): drawing the whole plot takes about 190 ms once; `refresh_cell` 0.06 ms; frames 1.0 ms at 0.5x and 0.7 ms at 1x idle; an edit outside a drag, with its validator rerun, about 8.5 ms (p95 frame 11.8 ms raising a 5x5 brush every frame); a 50-tile drag about 19 ms in all, since a drag reruns the validator once, when it ends.
- Land (D19): a new course is generated natural land (hills, tree stands, ponds, rough), split into parcels. The player starts owning a small block and buys parcels that border owned land from a land budget; building and holes go only on owned land. New sandbox stays as a separate mode on the D10 plot with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget. Settled by E6-S1 (D26 to D30): the numbers and rules are in Land, below.
- Out of bounds (D9, D29): OB is off the map, every `oob` tile, and, outside New sandbox, every tile on a parcel the player does not own. From E2-S3 `oob` is on the Build palette as OB ground, and course files already carry it (surface 8). Placed OB stakes stay in the Icebox. A ball that comes to rest OB costs one stroke and is dropped (Ball, Cup and hazards).
- Builder (`course/builder.gd`, the only terrain writer with `course/save.gd`): paint any of the 9 surfaces below (`oob` from E2-S3, D9), raise or lower one step per click, heights clamp at 0 and 7. One click, or one drag, is one undo step; a drag raises or lowers each tile once. Edits that change nothing are not recorded. Ctrl+Z (`ui_undo`) undoes. Active in Build mode only.
- Holes in Build (E2-S3): the Tee and Cup tools place hole 1's tee or cup on the tile clicked (a drag does not move them), through the builder. The first of them creates the hole, closed, with the par picked in the hole panel (3, 4 or 5; `default_par` 3 in `course/builder_tuning.tres` until the player picks). The par buttons set the hole's par. Tee, cup, par and Open are undo steps on the same stack as terrain, and an undo puts the holes back as that step found them, open flags included, then reruns the validator. The hole panel shows the yardage, the status (open or closed), Open (enabled only when the hole is closed and every rule passes) and every failing rule with its tile and reason; each failing rule's tile is highlighted on the map with its reason (`course/issue_marker.gd`): the diamond on the tile sorts with the terrain, so a hill in front hides it, while the badge, leader line and text box draw on top, laid out at the current zoom so neighbouring boxes never cover each other. A box is pushed up at most `issue_box_max_stack` (96 screen px, `course/builder_tuning.tres`) past its resting height; past that it moves sideways, at most `issue_box_max_side` (160 screen px); a box with no room within both limits collapses to its badge and diamond, and the hole panel still lists its reason (E2-S17). When a tile in front covers a fault's tile centre, its leader draws dashed and its foot and badge faded, so the fault does not read as being on the hill. Highlights and the panel show in Build only. A course brought into the builder with an open hole that fails a rule has that hole closed at once (not an undo step). A hole with only its tee or only its cup holds `HoleData.UNPLACED` (-1, -1) for the other, which `CourseData.from_dict` rejects: so `CourseSave.save_file` (and `to_json`) drops such a hole from the file (E2-S12): any hole whose tee or cup is off the map is left out, the rest of the course is saved, the course in memory is not changed, and the dropped hole numbers are returned through its `dropped` list so the caller can say so. The file loads back with the same `content_hash()` as the course with only its whole holes. The format has no unplaced value. Play is never gated by the validator (pillar 1): it is available whatever the hole's rules say; Open only decides whether guests may play it.
- Picking (`course/tile_picker.gd`): the cursor picks the tile drawn frontmost under it. Each tile is treated as a solid column from height 0 to its top, tested front to back by depth (i + j). A top face picks its tile, a wall picks the hill it belongs to, and a tile partly behind a nearer hill is picked only where its top still shows.
- Every surface has a pattern as well as a color (colorblind rule); patterns are named in `art/specs/surfaces.md`. Art uses only `art/palette.gpl` (32 colors). Terrain sheets load by name through `art/tiles/surfaces.tres`.

## Land

Settled on paper by E6-S1 (D26 to D30, user 2026-11-03), built in E6-S2 to E6-S8. New sandbox (D10) is unchanged by all of it: flat rough, every parcel owned, an unlimited budget, buying hidden.

- Parcels (D26): 16 x 16 tiles, 80 yd on a side, 6,400 sq yd (about 1.3 acres). Parcel (p, q) holds tiles x `16p` to `16p + 15` and y `16q` to `16q + 15`, clipped to the map, so a map of w x h tiles has `ceil(w / 16)` x `ceil(h / 16)` parcels and only a map whose size is not a multiple of 16 has partial ones. The 128 x 128 map (D10's size, so D10's draw and frame times hold) is 8 x 8 = 64 parcels. The parcel size is a course format constant, `CourseData.PARCEL_TILES` 16, not a tunable in `data/land.json`: format v2's owned flags are indexed by parcel, so a different size would read every saved file against the wrong grid. Changing it means a new format version with a `course/save.gd` migration.
- Ownership (D26) is part of CourseData, the single source of truth: one owned flag per parcel. Next to the flags CourseData holds the land budget left: a whole number of dollars, or unlimited (`null` in the file) for New sandbox, which is how a saved file tells a sandbox from a New course. Only `course/builder.gd` (a New course or New sandbox, a purchase, and its undo) and `course/save.gd` (a load) write either. Course format version 2 saves both, so a New course saved after two purchases reloads with what was left, not a fresh $5,000; `course/save.gd` loads a version 1 file with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget, the same as New sandbox, so every course and fixture made before E6 plays as before (E6-S3). Built in E6-S3: `CourseData` reads `parcels_wide`, `parcels_high`, `parcel_of`, `parcel_rect`, `is_parcel_owned`, `is_owned(x, y)` (off-map tiles are never owned), `land_budget` and `has_unlimited_budget()`; `CourseData.create` (New sandbox, fixtures) owns every parcel with no limit. In the file, `owned` is one string of `"0"` and `"1"` per parcel row and `land_budget` the dollars left or `null` (`.claude/skills/course-file/SKILL.md`); `CourseSave.migrate` turns a version 1 file into that. The builder buys through `buy_parcel` and says why not through `buy_refusal`; prices come from `course/land_prices.gd` (`LandPrices`, reading `data/land.json`).
- Starting block (D26): 2 x 2 parcels at the centre of the map, owned for free when a New course starts: parcels 3 and 4 on each axis of the 8 x 8, tiles 48 to 79 on both axes, 32 x 32 tiles, 160 yd on a side. The camera already frames the map's centre (Main), so the game opens on it.
- New course (E6-S6): `CourseBuilder.new_course(seed)` makes the generated land of that seed (`LandGenerator.generate`), owns the starting block and nothing else, and sets the land budget to `land_budget` in `data/land.json` ($5,000); no holes. The same seed gives the same course. The main scene starts on one (`Main.course_path` empty; a failed load warns and does the same), with the seed from `Main.start_seed` (-1, the default, picks one at random; `Main.land_seed` says which), and the camera frames the map's centre, where the block is, at 1x (`docs/decisions/e6-s6-new-course.png`). The Build HUD has New course (fresh land from a random seed) above New sandbox; each hands the course to a fresh builder (empty undo stack). New sandbox stays D10's flat rough plot with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget, and the Build palette hides Buy land on any course with an unlimited budget (New sandbox and every version 1 file, which own every parcel); a selected Buy land steps back to painting. The land panel still shows "Land budget: unlimited" there. Tests: `tests/test_new_course.gd`.
- Buying (D27): a parcel that is not owned may be bought when it shares an edge with an owned parcel (a shared corner only is not enough) and its price is no more than the land budget left. A refusal names the rule that failed: "not next to your land" or "costs $X, you have $Y" (money written as "$1,150"); a parcel already owned gives "already yours" and one off the map "no such parcel". With an unlimited budget only the edge rule applies and a purchase costs nothing. A purchase is one undo step on the builder's stack (E6-S3); undoing it gives the price back and the parcel up, and reruns the validator, so a hole that now stands on unowned land closes. Land is never sold back: there is no sell action, and undo is the only way a parcel is given up.
- Land budget (D28): $5,000 (`land_budget` in `data/land.json`) when a New course starts, spent on parcels only until E4-S1 folds it into the one balance (Economy and time). The starting block is free and does not come out of it. New sandbox has no budget limit.
- Parcel price (D28), kept as E6-S1 set it by E6-S8's `/balance land` (below). Read from the parcel's tiles: they are the generated land, since unowned land cannot be edited (E6-S4), so a parcel's price never changes before it is bought. All numbers in `data/land.json`:

  `price = base_price + relief_step_price × relief + tree_tile_price × trees + water_tile_price × water`, clamped to `min_price` to `max_price` and rounded to the nearest `price_round`.

  | Term | Value | Read as |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `base_price` | $1,000 | every parcel |
  | `relief_step_price` | +$100 per step | relief: the parcel's highest tile height minus its lowest (0 to 7), so hills cost more |
  | `tree_tile_price` | -$1 per tile | tree tiles on the parcel (0 to 256), ground to clear before a hole goes there |
  | `water_tile_price` | -$2 per tile | water tiles on the parcel, ground a hole plays round |
  | `min_price`, `max_price` | $500, $2,000 | clamp |
  | `price_round` | $50 | |

  Worked prices (hand calculation): flat open rough (relief 0, no trees or water) $1,000. A typical generated parcel at the rates below (relief 2, 31 trees, 10 water) is 1000 + 200 - 31 - 20 = 1149, $1,150. A hill parcel (relief 5, 20 trees) 1000 + 500 - 20 = 1480, $1,500. A wooded valley (relief 1, 80 trees, 20 water) 1000 + 100 - 80 - 40 = 980, $1,000. So $5,000 buys 4 typical parcels ($4,600) and leaves $400. Range: the generator's rough floor (at least 60% of a parcel, so at most 102 tree or water tiles) puts every generated parcel between relief 0 with 102 water, 1000 - 204 = 796, $800, and relief 7 with none, $1,700. So the $500 and $2,000 clamp only guards tuned values and partial parcels, and trees and water take at most about $100 to $200 off while relief adds up to $700: at these provisional numbers a wooded or wet parcel is only slightly cheaper. E6-S8's `/balance land` decides whether that is enough, and if bargain parcels should exist, raises the tree and water discounts rather than loosening the rough floor.

  Measured (E6-S8, `/balance land`, seeds 1 to 200): unowned parcels cost $900 to $1,600, median $1,050, 10th to 90th percentile $950 to $1,300, and none reaches the clamp; relief is mostly 0 to 3, so the typical parcel is cheaper than the hand calculation's $1,150. Trees and water take $52 off a parcel on average and $204 at most. The 8 parcels next to the block differ by $354 on average from dearest to cheapest, mostly by relief. Spent cheapest buyable parcel first, $5,000 buys 4 to 5 parcels (4.98 on average, $278 left), so a New course owns 8 or 9 parcels. Every target in Balance targets, land holds, so no number in `data/land.json` moved. The discounts stay small on purpose: until E4 painting is free, so clearing a tree or filling a pond costs nothing, and a bigger discount would pay the player to buy woods and clear them for free. E4-S1, which gives paint a cost, is where a wooded or wet parcel's discount should be set against what clearing it costs (Economy and time).
- A ball on unowned land (D29): out of bounds, as an `oob` tile is (D9, D15): a ball that touches down on or rolls onto a tile of an unowned parcel stops there, takes one penalty stroke and is dropped behind its entry point, and the drop walk passes over unowned tiles as it does over `oob` (`Ball.may_place_on` is false there). A flight over unowned land that comes down on owned land plays on. The user's answer was that a ball which comes to rest on unowned land is OB; measuring it as an `oob` tile (a ball that rolls onto unowned land is OB even if it would have rolled back) is the implementer's reading, chosen because existing OB tiles stop the ball that way, and E6-S8's hand playtest checks it (D29). `ShotPrediction` reads the same ownership, so the aim marker shows the OB outcome before the shot (pillar 2). The line between owned and unowned land is drawn as a boundary in Play and Build (E6-S5, E6-S7). Unowned land keeps its real heights, which the ball's height field still reads (a hill on unowned land still feeds a slope beside it). Built in E6-S7: `HeightField.surface_at` (and `surface_of(tile)`) reads every tile of an unowned parcel as `oob`, whatever is painted there, so the ball, `Ball.may_rest_on` and `may_place_on`, the drop walk, tree scatter, the lie and `ShotPrediction` all see the line through that one read, and a fully owned course (New sandbox, every version 1 file) reads every tile's own surface, so its play is unchanged tick for tick. Trees on unowned land still block a flight (E6-S7 review): the in-flight tree test (`Ball._blocks`, so the scatter, `ShotPrediction.stop_point` and the D20 blocked check) reads the painted surface (`HeightField.painted_surface_at`), because the trees are drawn there and trees past the stakes stop a real ball; touchdown, roll, rest and the drop still read unowned land as `oob`, and the scatter rests only on owned land. The land line in Play (E6-S7): `golf/land_boundary.gd` (`LandBoundary.segments`) lists every tile edge between an owned tile and an unowned one, walked parcel by parcel (the map's own edge is not one), at the higher tile top of the two; `scenes/aim_view.gd` draws it with the aim, before every shot, as chalk dashes on a solid ink line (white stakes: a pattern as well as a shade, and dashes, not the aim's dots), 2 px wide with 6 px dashes at 1x (`land_line_width`, `land_line_dash`, exported on AimView beside its other drawing sizes). Each tile edge carries a short tick pointing into the unowned side (`LandBoundary.unowned_side`, `land_tick_tiles` 0.3 tile), so the line says which side is out. AimView draws over the terrain, so an edge that a tile in front covers (`LandBoundary.covered`: a nearer tile's column crosses the line of sight to the edge's middle) draws faded (`land_line_hidden_alpha` 0.3), as an issue marker's leader does behind a hill (E2-S17), and the line does not read as running over the hill's top. The aim marker shows the OB outcome: when the prediction ends in water or OB (`penalty_strokes`), it draws the cue the struck ball shows there (ShotView: a flag_red circle struck through lower left to upper right for OB, chalk splash rings for water) at `ShotPrediction.hazard_position`, where the dots end; a putt draws it in place of the rest ring, and a full club in place of its landing ring when it lands in the hazard, or beside the ring when it lands clear and rolls in. Unowned land itself is drawn as painted in Play (E6-S5's dimming is Build only). Screenshots: `docs/decisions/e6-s7-land-line.png` (`tests/fixtures/land_line_shot.tscn`) and, with a hill in front of the line, `docs/decisions/e6-s7-land-line-hill.png` (`tests/fixtures/land_line_hill.tscn`). Tests: `tests/test_ball_land.gd`.
- What a hole needs to own (D30): its tee tile and its cup tile, and a rule-3 path, for which unowned land counts as OB. Its D11 footprint does not have to be owned: the line beside a hole is OB, the way a boundary fence runs beside a real hole, and buying the parcel beside it is how the hole gets room. A new validator rule 6 (E6-S4), "Tee and cup on owned land", reports the tee or the cup that stands on unowned land. The builder already refuses to place a tee or cup there (E6-S4), so the rule fires only after a purchase is undone or a file loads that way.
- Editing only owned land (E6-S4, D30): `course/builder.gd`'s paint, raise and lower write only the tiles of a brush that stand on owned parcels, so a brush or a drag that straddles the line edits its owned tiles and leaves the rest. A brush with tiles on the map and none owned, or a tee or cup placed (or moved) on an unowned tile, changes nothing, pushes no undo step and emits the builder's `refused` signal with "not your land"; `edit_refusal(center, size)` and `place_refusal(cell)` give the same reason before the click (E6-S5 shows it on the Build HUD). A brush off the map is not refused, it just edits nothing. Unowned land keeps its generated tiles, which is why a parcel's price never changes before it is bought. Rule 6 reports one issue on the tee tile and one on the cup tile, each that stands on unowned land, highlighted like any rule; rule 3's flood treats every tile of an unowned parcel as `oob` and names the first one on the line as "unowned land". No tunables.
- Showing land in Build (E6-S5, art/specs/land.md): every tile of an unowned parcel draws dimmed and hatched. The shade is a modulate, `unowned_tint` in `course/builder_tuning.tres` (0.62, 0.62, 0.68), on its top, risers and edge cues; the pattern, for the colorblind rule, is the land sheet's screen-diagonal hatch over its top, with a 2 px chalk line just inside each edge that lies on a parcel edge, so the owned/unowned boundary and the parcel grid both read. Both draw in Play too (the line is the OB boundary, D29); fully owned land (New sandbox) looks as before. The Build HUD's land panel (`scenes/land_panel.gd`) always shows the land budget ("Land budget: $5,000", or "unlimited"); hovering an unowned parcel shows its price and whether it can be bought ("Parcel 2, 3: $1,150, for sale (Buy land)" or "..., can't buy: not next to your land"). The palette's Buy land tool outlines the hovered parcel (yellow when it can be bought, red when not); a click asks to confirm ("Buy parcel 2, 3 for $1,150? $3,850 left after.") and only the confirm buys, through the builder; a click on a parcel that cannot be bought says why instead. Paint, raise, lower, tee and cup over a tile where they would do nothing draw their outline red and show "Paint: not your land" before the click (`CourseBuilder.edit_refusal`, `place_refusal`); a refused click shows the builder's `refused` reason ("Can't: not your land") for `refusal_show_s` (3 s, `course/builder_tuning.tres`). Screenshots in `docs/decisions/e6-s5-*.png` (`tests/fixtures/land_buy_shot.tscn`).
- Hand calculation: the starting block fits a par 3, not a par 4 (D26, D30). In tiles, the block's tile centres run 0 to 31 on each axis. Under D30 the tee and cup may be any two owned tiles, so the longest hole is corner to corner: `5 × sqrt(31² + 31²) = 5 × 43.84 = 219.2` yd, rounded 219, a par 3 (rule 4: up to 249). A par 4 needs 250 yd, more than the block's 219, so no par 4 fits, whatever the land. A 160 yd par 3 (the `/course-file generate 3` hole) fits: tee at block tile (0, 12) and cup at (31, 20) is `5 × sqrt(31² + 8²) = 160.08` yd, 160, both owned, with its rule-3 path along the line on owned land; or across the diagonal, tee (4, 4) to cup (27, 27), `5 × sqrt(23² + 23²) = 162.6` yd, 163, with 20 yd of owned land behind the tee and past the cup. Its footprint then reaches unowned land past the tee and the cup and, on the first layout, beside them, which D30 allows (one shot there is OB). Had D30 asked for the whole footprint, the tee and the cup would both have to sit on tiles 6 to 25 of each axis (a tile at index 5 is 6 tiles, 30 yd, from the unowned tiles at -1), and the longest hole would be `5 × sqrt(19² + 19²) = 134.4` yd: no 160 yd par 3, and a par 4 only on 2 x 4 parcels (`5 × sqrt(19² + 51²) = 272` yd), 4 purchases, against 1 under D30. One purchase that makes the block 2 x 3 parcels (32 x 48 tiles) gives `5 × sqrt(31² + 47²) = 281.5` yd: room for a par 4 of 250 to 281 yd, so the first purchase comes with the second hole, as D26 meant. Nine holes need about 34 parcels of footprint alone (a capsule of `(L + 60) × 60` sq yd per hole: a 160 yd par 3 2.1 parcels, a 380 yd par 4 4.1, a 520 yd par 5 5.4; three par 3s, four par 4s and two par 5s), far more than the block and the 4 parcels $5,000 buys, so E4's money has work to do (E6-S8's target).
- Generated land (E6-S2, rates set by E6-S1), on the 128 x 128 map from an integer seed: the same seed gives the same map. It uses only rough, trees and water, and heights 0 to 7; fairway, green, tee, bunker, cart path and `oob` are the player's to build. Rates, each a target E6-S2's test holds within its tolerance over seeds 1 to 20, tunables in `course/land_tuning.tres`:

  | What | Target | Tolerance |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Rough share of the map | the rest, about 84% | |
  | Trees share | 12%, in 15 to 35 stands of 20 to 200 tiles | 8% to 16% |
  | Water share | 4%, in 4 to 10 ponds of 15 to 120 tiles | 2% to 6% |
  | Base height (D10's lowland) | 2 | |
  | Hills | 4 to 8 per map, peaks 2 to 5 steps above the base (so up to 7) | heights 0 to 7 always |
  | Tiles at height 4 or more | 15% | 8% to 25% |
  | Steepest generated slope | one step between edge neighbours, at most one riser every 2 tiles along either axis | exact |
  | Ponds | flat, one step below their lowest shore tile, never below 0 | exact |
  | Every parcel | at least 60% of its tiles rough, and never all water | exact |
  | Starting block | at least 85% rough and no water, so the first par 3 needs no pond | exact |

  Why one riser every 2 tiles at most: long rough slopes of one step per tile settle in up to 14.8 s, over D16's 10 s off-green target (Ball, Known limits, E6-S10), while slopes stepping every 2 tiles settle under 7 s on rough. So generated land meets D16 before E6-S10, and steeper ground is the player's own doing. The generator is meant to replace `/course-file generate hilly`'s ridge (D19, E2-S18); until a story does that, `generate hilly` keeps its one-step ridge (`CourseGenerator.ridge_rise`).

  How it is built (E6-S2, `course/land_generator.gd`, `LandGenerator.generate(seed)`, about 45 ms on 128 x 128): one `RandomNumberGenerator` seeded with the seed, three passes in order. It never writes a CourseData: it builds the tiles and loads them through `CourseData.from_dict`, as `CourseGenerator` does.
  - Hills: `hill_count_min` to `hill_count_max` (4 to 8) hills at random centres, each a cosine bell `peak` steps high (`hill_peak_min` to `hill_peak_max`, 2 to 5) and `peak × hill_radius_per_step` tiles wide (5 to 8 tiles per step), plus 0 to `hill_lobes_max` (2) smaller lobes `hill_lobe_offset` (0.45 to 0.85) of its radius off its centre at `hill_lobe_scale` (0.45 to 0.8) of its peak and radius, so hills are not round. The bells are summed by their highest, rounded to whole steps on 2 x 2 tile terrace cells, and clamped so neighbouring cells differ by one step at most: a riser can only fall on a cell edge, which is what makes the slope rule exact. Every radius is then scaled by one factor between `hill_scale_min` and `hill_scale_max` (0.6 to 1.6), found by bisection, so that `high_ground_share` (15%) of the map is at `high_ground_level` (4) or more; on seeds 1 to 20 it lands on 15.0%.
  - Ponds: 6 to 10 ponds (`ponds_min` to `ponds_max`, raised to at least `water_share` over `pond_max_tiles`), each sized to share what is left of the `water_share` target (±30%, within `pond_min_tiles` to `pond_max_tiles`). A pond grows by 2 x 2 squares from a random square, never leaving a one-tile gap along an axis, on rough ground that is flat for 2 tiles all round and at height 1 or more, outside the starting block, and is then dug one step. A pond that cannot reach `pond_min_tiles` is dropped and tried elsewhere.
  - Tree stands: 15 to 35 stands (`tree_stands_min` to `tree_stands_max`, kept where `tree_share` over the stand sizes allows), each sized to share what is left of the `tree_share` target (±40%, within `tree_stand_min_tiles` to `tree_stand_max_tiles`), grown on rough one random neighbouring tile at a time, never touching another stand, so each stand is one patch.
  - Floors: a pond square or a tree tile that would take a parcel under `parcel_min_rough` (60%) rough, or the starting block under `start_block_min_rough` (85%), is not laid.
  - Every number above is in `course/land_tuning.tres`; `tests/test_land_generator.gd` holds the table's targets over seeds 1 to 20 (and held them over 1 to 150 when written). The 2 x 2 terrace cell and the 2-tile flat margin round a pond are structural (`LandGenerator.TERRACE`, `POND_MARGIN`), not tunables: they are what the slope and pond rules rest on. `tests/fixtures/land_shot.tscn` shows a seed in Build (`LAND_SEED`, `LAND_ZOOM`, `LAND_FOCUS`).

## Hole validation rules (each is a GUT test)

1. Exactly one tee and one cup.
2. Cup sits on a green tile.
3. A path of non-water, non-OB tiles connects tee to cup.
4. Tee-to-cup yardage matches par: par 3 < 250, par 4 250 to 470, par 5 > 470.
5. No tee inside another hole's landing zone.
6. Tee and cup on owned land (D30, E6-S4; New sandbox owns every parcel, so it always passes there).

How each rule is measured (D11). Distances are between tile centres at 5 yd per tile. `course/validator.gd` (E2-S2) is the one implementation, called by the game and by `/course-file validate` (`course/course_file.gd`). Each failing rule returns the tile at fault and a one-line reason.

- Rule 1, one tee and one cup (E2-S2): a hole fails when its tee or cup is not placed yet (off the map, `HoleData.UNPLACED`; the other rules are then not measured), when its tee and cup are the same tile, or when another hole has the same number (a second tee and cup for that hole; each reports the other's tee). A placed tee or cup is the tile at fault.

- Rule 2, cup on green: the cup's tile is the tile at fault.
- Rule 3, path: tiles connect through their 4 edge neighbours (no diagonals). Every in-map surface except water and `oob` may be crossed, trees included. Heights are ignored. A tile on an unowned parcel counts as `oob` (D30, E6-S4). The tee tile and the cup tile are part of the path. The tile at fault is the first water or `oob` tile on a 4-connected walk along the tee-to-cup line (E2-S2); a walk with none would itself be a path, so there always is one.
- Rule 4, yardage: the straight line from tee centre to cup centre, `5 * sqrt(dx² + dy²)` yd, rounded to the nearest whole yard (`roundi`). The rounded number is the one the card shows, and par is checked on it: par 3 up to 249, par 4 250 to 470 (both included), par 5 471 and up. The raw value is never exactly x.5 (`sqrt(n) = (2k + 1) / 10` has no whole n), so rounding has no tie. The cup is the tile at fault. Boundary cases for the tests: (50, 0) and (30, 40) tiles apart are 250 yd, par 4; (47, 17) is 249.90, rounds to 250, par 4; (49, 9) is 249.10, par 3; (94, 0) is 470, par 4; (94, 4) is 470.43, par 4; (94, 5) is 470.66, rounds to 471, par 5.
- Rule 5, landing zone: one disc for each par 4 and par 5. Its centre is on the tee-to-cup line, `min(240 yd, yardage - 60 yd)` from the tee centre (190 yd or more, since a par 4 is at least 250 yd). Its radius is 30 yd (6 tiles). A tee is inside when its tile centre is 30 yd or less from the centre. A par 3 has no zone: its tee shot is aimed at the green, and a next tee beside the green is ordinary routing. The 60 yd cup clearance puts the disc's far edge 30 yd short of the cup, so a tee less than 30 yd from a hole's cup is never inside that hole's zone. Test cases: a 150 yd par 3 from tile (10, 10) to (40, 10) with the next tee at (44, 13), 25 yd from its cup, passes; a 260 yd par 4 (centre 200 yd out) with another tee 25 yd beside its cup passes (65 yd from the centre). 240 yd sits inside the driver target (230 to 260, Balance targets), and 30 yd at 240 yd is a 7.1 degree cone either way, the curve of a swing 35% off centre (2 degrees per 10%). Rule 5 checks pairs: when hole B's tee is inside hole A's zone, both A and B fail it, and each reports B's tee tile. Placing or moving any tee or cup reruns it for every hole.
- Footprint (an edit there closes an open hole, design doc "Undo"): every tile whose centre is within 30 yd of the tee-to-cup segment, a capsule that holds the landing zone. A surface or height edit on any footprint tile, or moving the hole's own tee or cup, closes the hole, even if it still passes. It stays closed until the player presses Open again, which reruns the validator. `CourseValidator.in_footprint` is the one implementation, read by `course/builder.gd`.
- Rerun after every edit: the footprint does not hold everything the rules read. Rule 3's path may leave it, and rule 5 reads other holes' tees. So after every build edit (a surface or height edit, or placing or moving any tee or cup) the validator also reruns for every open hole, and any open hole that now fails a rule closes, with that rule highlighted. Two cases for the tests: a lake painted across the whole capsule, with the rule-3 path going round its end outside the capsule, then water painted on that path, closes the hole; moving hole B's tee into open hole A's zone closes A (A and B both fail rule 5). The footprint check closes a hole on any edit there; the rerun closes it only when a rule fails. The rerun is one 4-neighbour search per open hole over at most 16,384 tiles (D10). The builder runs it after every click edit, tee, cup or par change and undo; a drag (one undo step) closes footprints on every tile it touches but reruns the rules once, when it ends.
- Tunables: the landing distance (240 yd), the cup clearance (60 yd), the zone radius (30 yd) and the footprint half width (30 yd) live in `course/validator_tuning.tres` (E2-S2), with rule 4's par bands as the shortest par 4 (250 yd) and the shortest par 5 (471 yd). Change them with the driver target, not alone.
- Hole markers (E2-S2): the course view draws a tee marker (two square sun_yellow blocks either side of the tile centre) and a pin (an ink cup, a chalk pole and a flag_red pennant) for every placed tee and cup in CourseData, at the centre of their tile's top (`Iso` height = the tile's whole height), Y-sorted as objects on that tile (`Iso.object_sort_key`). Drawn in code (`course/hole_marker.gd`) until the final art.
- Generated courses (E2-S2): `/course-file generate <par> [flat|hilly] [water|dry]` lays out one straight hole along +x on rough at height 2 (D10): a 3x3 tee box, a 5-wide fairway from 40 yd out, a round green (radius 3.2 tiles) round the cup, at 160, 380 or 520 yd for par 3, 4 or 5; `water` adds a pond 3 tiles long across the fairway at 60% of the way, dug one step, with dry ground round its ends. `courses/example_par3.json` is `generate 3 flat dry`, and a test keeps it equal to the generator. `hilly` (E2-S18) adds a gentle ridge: every column from 35% of the way to the cup for 4 tiles (20 yd) along the hole sits one step above the plot, across the whole map, with one-step risers either side (D5); the tee box, green and pond stay at their flat heights, so the surfaces and the rules are the flat hole's (`CourseGenerator.ridge_rise`, kept on its own so E6-S2's land generator can replace it, D19). Fixture shapes, constants in `course/course_generator.gd`, not tunables. `/course-file inspect <path>` (E2-S18) prints the size, the height range, the tile count of each surface present, and each hole's par, yardage, open or closed and validator PASS or FAIL with its failing rules; build cost joins it when tile costs get a resource (E4).

## Swing

- Three-click bar: start, power, accuracy. Bar fills in 1.2 s, runs to 110%. Kept at its E2-S7 numbers after the E2-S9 hand playtest (D24).
- Accuracy: each 10% off center = 2 degrees hook or slice. Outside zone = shank.
- The bar (E2-S7, `golf/swing.gd`, no scene; numbers in `golf/swing_tuning.tres`, tuned by hand in E2-S9). Click 1 starts the bar from 0; it reaches 100% in `fill_time` 1.2 s at bar speed 1.0 and runs on to `max_power` 110%, where it locks by itself. Click 2 locks the power at the bar, and the bar runs back at the same speed past the accuracy centre (0) to `bar_min` -30%. Click 3 locks the accuracy: the bar's position then, positive when early (above the centre), negative when late. Reaching `bar_min` with no third click is a late miss.
- Accuracy zone: `zone_half` 20% either side of the centre at 100% power or less (edges count as inside), so up to 4 degrees of curve. Early turns the heading right on screen (+heading, a slice for a right-hander), late turns it left (a hook), `deg_per_10pct` 2 degrees per 10% (D14: a heading offset at launch, the ball then flies straight). Overswing penalty: above 100% the zone narrows linearly to `overswing_zone_scale` 0.5 of its width at 110% (10% either side); the carry still grows to 1.1 times. Shank (outside the zone): the heading turns `shank_deg` 30 degrees toward the side of the miss, and the power is multiplied by `shank_power` 0.5. Every number is deterministic, so a swing's result is known at the third click.
- Bar speed (accessibility): a setting on the Play swing panel, `bar_speed_min` 0.5 to `bar_speed_max` 1.5 (default 1.0); the bar moves at `bar_speed / fill_time` per second, so 100% takes 2.4 s at the slowest and 0.8 s at the fastest. The accuracy return uses the same speed.
- Putter mapping: the starting ground speed is linear across the putter's range, `putt_min_speed + (putt_max_speed - putt_min_speed) × power`, 0% the slowest (1 yd/s) and 100% the fastest (18 yd/s). Overswing adds no speed (it is clamped at 100%), but its narrower zone still applies, as does a shank (half power, 30 degrees). The accuracy offset turns a putt's heading as it does a flight's.
- Play input (`scenes/swing_control.gd`, `scenes/swing_panel.gd`, `scenes/swing_bar.gd`, `scenes/aim_view.gd`): `swing` (Space) or a left click on the map is a swing click, and the click does not re-aim (the motion before it already did); the mouse aims at the tile the player sees under it while no swing is in progress (`TilePicker.pick`, the same top-surface pick Build uses, unprojected at that tile's height for the point on its top; a riser face aims at its hill tile's centre; off the map, read at the ball's height); `aim_left` and `aim_right` (arrow keys) turn the aim by `aim_key_step_deg` 1 degree per press (held keys repeat), and after a key turn the mouse takes the aim back only once it moves `mouse_reaim_px` 24 screen px from where it was (or from the next ball); `club_prev` and `club_next` (up and down arrows, or the panel's buttons) step through the clubs the lie and stroke allow (Club legality, below), longest first, which is the default. Entering Play puts the ball on hole 1's tee aimed at its cup, or on the debug shot's tile along its heading when there is no tee; the next swing starts where the struck ball rests, or at its drop point after water or OB (E2-S8). The debug shot fires from the `debug_shot` key (F3) in Play, in debug builds only (E2-S8; a HUD button until then); it cannot fire while a swing is in progress or a ball is moving (`Main.can_fire_debug_shot`), and its ball never moves the swing ball or counts a stroke (SwingControl rests only a stroke it struck itself). After a debug shot the debug ball stays drawn where it rests until the next swing.
- Aim line and marker (`scenes/aim_view.gd`): from the aim's ShotPrediction (a perfect swing, the next stroke's seed), refreshed when the club, aim or ball changes. A full club: dots every 2.5 yd (measured on the ground) on the straight screen segment from the drawn ball to the marker, the 3D chord the straight flight takes between them (E2-S7 review: dots on the ground started at the foot of a raised tee and climbed the hill as if the shot ran along it), and the marker (a sun_yellow ring with four ticks and a centre dot) at the first touchdown. The putter: dots along the predicted roll at its bilinear ground height, so the break reads as a smooth curve (tile-top dots jumped 16 px at every tile edge of a contour, larger than the break), and the marker at the rest point. The putt shown is the one at the pace that rests nearest the cup (D21, Aim marker below), not a 100% putt. The marker stands on the top of the tile under it (D17's rule, `HeightField.tile_top_screen`), as do the ball and shadow on the ground and the struck flight's red landing cross, so a perfect swing's cross sits exactly on the ring. Everything draws over the terrain; below 1x they keep their 1x size on screen. Hidden while the ball moves; a struck flight shows its predicted landing as the red cross (E1-S7), a putt shows none. A blocked club (D20, Shot model) shows a flag_red ring struck through by a slash where its flight is stopped and no dots.
- Clubs: driver, 3w, 3i to 9i, PW, SW, putter. Each: carry, launch angle, roll factor. Data in `golf/clubs.tres` (`golf/club.gd`, `golf/club_set.gd`). Starting values (E2-S5), set for a ball with no drag against the clubs targets; tune with `/balance clubs`. Carry is a perfect swing's flat carry from a 1.0 lie; the launch speed follows from it (Shot model).

Putter (E2-S5): starting ground speed 1.0 to 18.0 yd/s (`putt_min_speed`, `putt_max_speed`); the bar maps linearly across it (E2-S7, Swing above). A full putt (18 yd/s) on a flat green rolls 27.5 yd, so N = 30 yd (`full_putt_max_yd` in `golf/clubs.tres`): a putt can cross a 6-tile green, and 30 yd leaves room for the E2-S4 green friction change.
- Lies: fairway/tee 1.0, rough 0.8 to 0.9, bunker 0.5. Uphill shortens carry; sidehill adds curve. Data in `golf/lies.tres` (`golf/lie_table.gd`, read into a `golf/lie.gd`). Starting values (E2-S5), tuned with `/balance lies`: rough 0.85; `uphill_k` 0.037 per step of rise (a 2-step lie costs the 7-iron 10.8 yd); `sidehill_deg` 2.9 degrees per step of tilt (a 1-step lie curves the 7-iron 7.4 yd at 146 yd); the windows `ahead_yd` and `side_yd` are D14's 5 yd. Water, trees and `oob` read 1.0, since a ball never rests there (E2-S6); green reads 1.0, since only the putter plays there.

Shot model (D14). E2-S5 sets the numbers, E2-S7 builds the swing on it. Code (E2-S5): `golf/shot_model.gd` (`ShotModel.full`, `ShotModel.putt`) builds a `golf/shot_launch.gd` for a stroke, with no scene; `ShotLaunch.apply` sends a Ball off, and `ShotPrediction.run_launch` predicts it with the same step.

- Power is a share of carry, not of launch speed (design doc "Power"). A full club's target carry is `carry × power × lie`, where `lie` is the surface multiplier times the uphill multiplier. The launch speed is the one whose flat-ground carry at the club's launch angle equals that target. With no drag (Ball) that is `v_full × sqrt(power × lie)`, since flat carry is `v² sin(2θ) / g`. 110% overswing is 1.1 times the carry, before E2-S7's penalty.
- Surface lie multipliers scale carry, as the Distance column says, through that one product. Rough is one fixed value from 0.8 to 0.9 (start 0.85, a 15% loss), never rolled, so the same lie always gives the same marker. Bunker is 0.5, and the sand wedge is the only club there.
- Lie slope is measured at the rest point `p`, in steps, with heights from `HeightField.height_at`. Rise (uphill) looks ahead only: `(h(p + 5 yd along the aim) - h(p)) / 2 yd per step`. Ground behind the ball never counts, so flat ground ahead reads 0 wherever the ball rests. Tilt (sidehill) is centred across the aim, a 10 yd window: `(h(p + 5 yd to the left of the aim) - h(p + 5 yd to the right)) / 2 yd per step`, whose sign names the higher side. Ground on both sides counts, since a sidehill lie is ground falling across the stance, so a ball on flat ground beside a bank reads a tilt away from the bank. A rise above 0 multiplies carry by `1 - k × rise`, and a rise of 0 or below does nothing (lower ground ahead already lengthens the flight). A tilt turns the launch heading toward the lower side by `c` degrees per step of tilt. Left is as the player sees it on screen: the projection draws +y clockwise from +x, so left is the aim heading minus 90 degrees, and a positive tilt (left higher) adds to the heading. E2-S5 sets `k` and `c` against the lies targets. Lie slope and the surface multiplier apply to full clubs only, never to the putter. Why a window and not the gradient at `p`: off the green a ball rests only where the slope's pull is under the still-ball friction, a gradient under 0.14 (fairway, 0.36 steps per tile) or 0.25 (rough, 0.62), while a one-step rise between two tile centres is 0.4 across its whole band (only where a rise fades out at a corner is it gentler). So the gradient under a resting ball is almost always near 0, and a slope lie read from it would almost never happen. The window sees the rise just ahead of the ball, so it depends on how far short of a riser the ball rests. On flat ground facing a 2-step riser, the rise is 2 at the tile centre where the riser's band begins (a 2-step uphill lie), and `2 × (5 - d) / 5` at `d` yd short of it, 0 from 5 yd back. The lies targets name the spot for that reason. Rise and tilt count steps of height, not gradient, so `k` and `c` are per step whatever the window's length (hand calculation step 6).
- Hook, slice and sidehill curve are heading offsets at launch, added to the aim: 2 degrees per 10% off centre (Accuracy), plus the sidehill turn. The ball then flies straight. Ball gets no new term.
- A club's roll factor multiplies the ground speed the ball keeps after its first touchdown, after the surface's bounce friction. It is `Ball.roll_factor`, set at launch (E2-S5). It changes nothing else: the surface's Roll, bounce, scatter and still-ball friction stay as the surface table says. Scaling Roll instead would scale the still-ball friction too. On rough a roll factor under 0.68 would then make that friction (2.5 / factor yd/s²) beat the one-step pull (3.69 yd/s²), and balls would stop rolling down slopes (the GUT slope guard). On green it would move D7's hold. The putter has no roll factor: a putt starts rolling (`Ball.start_roll`).
- Putter: the bar sets the putt's starting ground speed across the putter's range (E2-S5 sets the range, E2-S7 the mapping: linear, Swing above), not a carry.
- Aim marker: for a full club, the first touchdown of a predicted perfect swing (100% power, accuracy at centre) with the selected club and lie, including rough, rise and tilt. For the putter (D21, E2-S20), the rest point of the putt, accuracy at the centre, at the power whose putt comes to rest nearest the cup (`PuttAim`, `golf/putt_aim.gd`); with no cup on the map, a putt at 100%. Both come from the same Ball step as the flight (E1-S7), so a perfect swing lands within 1 tile of the marker, and a putt struck at the shown power rests on it. The bar's locked power still decides the putt, so the marker shows the line and break of the putt the player needs, and the player still has to find its pace. The putter's power is solved, not scanned: a safeguarded secant on the signed miss along the aim (yards past the cup's projection onto the aim, negative short; a cup square to the aim or behind it shows the 0% putt; E2-S20 review: measured along the ball-to-cup line, an aim turned 60 degrees off the cup showed a putt 0.6 yd further from it than the nearest the bar can make, and 3.4 yd at 90 degrees), from a first guess of distance / `full_putt_max_yd` (or the last solve's power while the ball stays put), stopping at a holed putt, a miss within `putt_aim_tolerance_yd` 0.1 yd, the bar's edge (a cup out of reach shows the 100% putt), or `putt_aim_max_runs` 8 predicted putts (`golf/swing_tuning.tres`); of every putt run it keeps the one resting nearest the cup, a dry rest before a drop. Cost (E2-S20, `tests/test_shot_prediction.gd`): a putt runs about 36 µs per tick, 2 to 11 ms to rest; sweeping the aim on the contour, 1.2 putts and about 9 ms per aim update (worst 2); a cold start (a new ball position) 3 putts, about 22 ms. Worst cases measured in the E2-S20 review: sweeping -20 to +20 degrees on the contour, 2 to 3 putts and 15 to 21.5 ms per update (over a 16.7 ms frame, so the aim can stutter); a cold start over 126 ball positions on the contour and its mirror, up to 3 putts and 36 ms; on a flat green swept 25 to 65 degrees off the cup, 3 to 4 putts; on a green rising two steps per tile, a cold start up to the 8-putt cap and 60 to 173 ms, since its putts settle for up to 12.7 s (about 27 ms a run). Limit: the solve assumes the miss grows with power; on a bank that steep a harder uphill putt can roll back further, and the marker can sit up to about 4 yd further from the cup than the nearest putt the bar can make (18 of 100 cold solves more than 0.5 yd off). On the flat and the contour it is within 0.35 yd. Screenshot: `docs/decisions/e2-s20-putt-marker.png` (`tests/fixtures/putt_marker_shot.tscn`).
- The putter's marker (E2-S7 review, closed by D21 in E2-S20): a 100% putt rolls 27.5 yd on a flat green, so on `courses/example_par3.json` the old 100% marker sat 23.2, 21.0 and 17.5 yd past the cup for putts from 2, 5 and 10 yd short, and its dots showed the break of a roll nobody plays. The user chose a marker at the power that reaches the cup; built as the Aim marker line above says.
- A ball at the foot of a riser (D20, E2-S10): the physics is kept. The flight starts at the ball's ground height and its first tick is checked against the ground, so a club whose launch is flatter than the face ahead meets it at once and its carry (the first touchdown) is about 0. A one-step riser's band is a 0.4 slope (21.8 degrees) and a two-step riser in one tile is 0.8 (38.7 degrees). Blocked rule: a full club is blocked when its predicted perfect swing (the aim's ShotPrediction, the same Ball step) meets the ground or trees while still climbing (`ShotPrediction.stopped_climbing`, `ClubSet.is_blocked`): a riser face or trees ahead stop it before its apex. Every flight that gets away comes down descending, so the rule has no distance in it, and it reads the flight before any tree scatter, so it does not change with the stroke's seed. A putt is never blocked. A blocked club stays legal and can still be struck; its marker sits where the flight is stopped (`ShotPrediction.stop_point`: the first touchdown on a face, or the tree edge, not the scatter point behind the ball), drawn as a flag_red ring struck through by a slash, with no dots, and the club picker reads "Club: <club> (blocked)" in flag_red, with a tooltip. Measured from the tile centre where the band begins, 100% power, lie on, seed 1 (`tests/test_clubs.gd`, `test_a_club_flatter_than_the_riser_ahead_meets_it_at_once_and_is_blocked` and `test_the_blocked_rule_holds_further_back_from_a_riser`): on one step the driver, 3w, 3i, 4i and 5i carry under 2 yd and are blocked, and the 6i to SW fly (6i 147 yd, SW 96 yd); 1 yd further back the 4i and 5i fly and the driver, 3w and 3i still carry under 5 yd (blocked); 2 yd back the driver (5.1 yd) and 3w (6.6 yd) are blocked; 3 yd back the driver (7.6 yd); 4 yd back nothing. On two steps every club carries under 5 yd and is blocked, the SW included (4.4 yd); 2 yd back the driver to 8i, 4 yd back the driver to 6i, 7 yd back the driver, 3w and 3i, 10 yd back the driver (14.2 yd into the face), 15 yd back nothing. Every club in those cases is either blocked (carry under 15 yd) or flies over 90 yd. Trees (`test_trees_right_in_front_block_every_club_on_every_seed`, a band three tiles deep): 0 to 2 tiles past the tee every club is blocked on seeds 1 to 10; 6 tiles out the driver to 4i are (still climbing at about 30 yd) and the 5i to SW clear them; 10 tiles out none is. A blocked ball does not always stop: the driver and 3w bounce off the face and run up and over it (about 110 and 80 yd total on one step, up to 130 yd from further back), while the 8i, 9i and PW on two steps roll back behind the ball; the blocked marker says the shot is not a flight to the marker, so it no longer has to match. Every club that is not blocked flies, and its marker is its first touchdown (`tests/test_shot_prediction.gd`, `test_the_marker_and_picker_show_a_club_blocked_at_the_foot_of_a_riser`). Guests (E3) choose clubs with `ClubSet.is_blocked` and skip a blocked one. The lies targets fly the lie on flat fairway (Balance targets).

Club legality (D13), checked in this order for each stroke:

1. On green: the putter only.
2. In a bunker: the sand wedge only.
3. Anywhere else: every club but the putter. The driver is allowed only on the hole's first stroke, from its tee marker, or from any tee tile. So a tee marker placed on fairway still allows the driver, and a ball that comes to rest on a tee tile later may use it too. The sand wedge is allowed on every lie except green.

Scoring (D12): stroke play. A hole's score counts strokes plus penalty strokes. The ball is picked up when that count reaches double par (6 on a par 3, 8 on a par 4, 10 on a par 5) without the ball holed, including when a penalty stroke reaches it. ## Ball

- Parabola in 3D over height map, 60 Hz, deterministic by seed.
- One bounce on landing by surface, then roll down slope with surface friction.
- Water and OB: one-stroke penalty, drop at last entry point (Cup and hazards, D15).
- Code: `golf/ball.gd` (Ball, RefCounted, no scene) over `golf/height_field.gd`. Units are yards and seconds, x and y on the ground from the map corner, z up. Randomness comes only from the ball's own seeded generator.
- Height between tiles is bilinear between tile centres, so a one-step rise is a slope from one centre to the next. Past the map edge the edge height holds. The slope along an axis jumps at each tile centre on that axis; exactly on a centre it is the mean of the slopes on both sides (E2-S4), so a pit's floor line is level and a ball set on a rim's centre line feels half the wall.
- A flight that meets the ground (including a hillside) touches down at the contact point found inside the tick. That first contact is the landing position.

Starting values from E1-S6, in `golf/ball_params.tres`. Tune with `/balance`:

- Yards per height step: 2.0 (14 yards at height 7). Matches the 16 px riser on a 64x32 tile at 5 yards per tile.
- Gravity: 10.7 yd/s² (Earth). There is no drag yet, so carry comes from launch speed and angle alone. A 75 yd/s drive at 14 degrees carries about 247 yd and finishes about 296 yd on fairway (299 before E6-S10's fairway `drag_scale` 1.2).
- Rolling friction has two parts at roll factor 1.0: a constant 1.5 yd/s² (`roll_decel`) plus grass drag of 0.6 per second times the speed (`roll_drag`). A surface's friction is that divided by its Roll column, and roll 0 stops the ball where it touches down. The drag stops fast balls; the small constant lets slow balls feel the slope. Per surface (E1-S11), `drag_scale` multiplies the drag, and `rest_decel_scale` multiplies the constant for a still ball, rising linearly back to the full constant at `decel_ramp_speed`. Both are 1.0 except on green (see the table below), and `drag_scale` is 1.1 on cart path (E2-S4 review: with the lip no longer braking a ball, a driver down a cart path stepping 7 to 0 took 10.0 to 10.4 s on the ground; 1.1 brings every such drive under 10 s, the flat 100% drive from 9.2 s to 8.7 s and its roll-out past the carry from 182 to 175 yd). Per surface (E2-S4), `drag_full_speed` fades the drag in proportion to speed below it, so a slow ball running down a slope is not held to a crawl: 12 yd/s on rough, 0 (full drag at every speed) elsewhere. Without it a rough slope of one step per tile let a ball reach only 1.2 yd/s, and a still ball took 18.7 s to reach the floor of a 4-step rough valley. On green the still-ball constant is also the hold that keeps a slow ball on a gentle slope (D7, next lines), and ramping it back to full for moving balls keeps fast putts as they were. Fairway and tee `drag_scale` is 1.2 since E6-S10 (a 10 yd/s ball runs 8.7 yd on flat fairway instead of 9.8, still short of the green's 13.1; a test guards it). Downhill (E6-S10), per surface: `downhill_friction_scale` is the share of the rolling friction, constant part and drag alike, a ball keeps where the slope pulls it on along its line. The friction falls toward it in proportion to that pull along the ball's line over the constant friction (`Ball.downhill_share`), so in full on every slope of a step per tile or more off the green, and not at all on flat ground, straight across a slope or uphill: flat rolls and climbs are as before. 0.25 on fairway, tee and rough, 1.0 on cart path and green (Ball, Known limits, long slopes off the green).
- Slope pull along the ground is gravity × felt gradient / (1 + felt gradient²), at most gravity / 2, where the felt gradient is the terrain's gradient times the surface's `slope_scale`. The ground normal at touchdown tilts by the same felt gradient. The ball's height always follows the real terrain. A one-step slope pulls about 3.7 yd/s² at slope_scale 1.0 and 0.32 yd/s² on green (0.075, so a 40% step putts as a 3% break). Off the green the one-step pull must stay above the still-ball friction, `roll_decel` × `rest_decel_scale` / roll, on every rolling surface (a GUT test guards it), or balls never start down a slope. On green that friction is 0.48 yd/s² by design (D7, E1-S12): it holds a ball on a felt slope up to about 4.5%, so a one-step contour (3%, or 4.2% where two meet at a corner) holds it, while a green rising two steps per tile still rolls it down (a GUT test guards both sides). Steep green banks (E2-S4, D16, D22; retuned in E2-S11 and E6-S11): on each axis, the part of the terrain's slope above `steep_from` (0.4, one step per tile) is felt at `steep_scale` (20) on top of `slope_scale`, never more than the terrain's own slope. So every one-step contour, corners included (0.4 on each axis), putts exactly as before, while any axis steeper than about 1.05 steps per tile (0.42) is felt in full: two steps per tile as 80% instead of 6%, three as 120%. Just past a contour the felt slope already pulls a still ball well over the D7 hold (0.41 on one axis: 2.3 yd/s² against 0.48; 0.5 on one axis is felt in full, 4.3 yd/s²), so a ball does not creep where a slope is barely steeper than a contour (E2-S11: from E2-S4's `steep_from` 0.6 and `steep_scale` 1.5, the rim of a two-step green pit crept for up to 20.7 s). E6-S11 raised `steep_scale` from 3.0 to 20: at 3.0 a slope between 0.4 and about 0.46 pulled only a little over the hold, and since the hold rises back to the full constant friction by `decel_ramp_speed`, a ball there ran at a terminal speed of 0.3 to 0.6 yd/s for several seconds (random greens, Known limit below). Nothing else moved: the bank, pit, trench, gully and climb cases in Settle targets measure the same or less. A friction change alone cannot settle a steep green: at a felt 6% the pull is 0.64 yd/s², so even with no friction a still ball at the top of a two-steps-per-tile bank needs 6.7 s to reach its foot and over 2 s more to stop. Per axis, not by the slope's length, so a gentle gully floor between steep green walls stays a gentle break.
- Creases (E2-S4, per axis since its review): where the ground under a rolling ball turns up against it along an axis, at a tile centre (a valley or pit floor, the foot of an upslope), the ball loses that axis's part of its 3D velocity along the new ground's normal, as rigid ground held to the ball would take it. On that axis it keeps (1 + old slope × new slope) / (1 + new slope²) of its speed: 72% across a one-step V floor at full slope, all of it onto a flat foot. A crease never adds speed and never turns the ball back: past a 90 degree crease (walls steeper than 45 degrees, a pit or trench three or more steps deep) that axis stops, where reflecting it set up a 0.3 yd/s swing over the floor until the 60 s cap. At a convex crease (a lip, or onto a steeper downhill) nothing is lost, so a ball over a drop runs at least as far as on flat ground (a 20 yd/s fairway ball rests at x 45.6 to 46.5 over a 1- to 7-step drop against 43.9 on flat). The crease feels the felt slope only across a low point (falling, then rising, on that axis), so a steep green pit or gully floor damps the swing in full; elsewhere it feels `slope_scale` of the terrain's slope, so on green a steep bank pulls a ball back hard but does not take a putt's speed at its foot: a putt climbs a one-tile riser of 2 to 7 steps from 1 yd short at 9.4 to 10.3 yd/s and from 7.5 yd short at 13.1 to 14.0 yd/s (one step: 6.1 and 10.1; E2-S11, from 9.1 to 10.5 and 12.8 to 14.1 before). This is what damps a ball swinging across a valley floor; the friction alone took up to 12.9 s on cart path. Across a low point on an axis (falling, then rising: a valley or pit floor, not the foot of a bank or a lip), a ball also keeps only the surface's `floor_keep` of what that leaves it (E6-S10), as a ball thuds into the far wall: 0.4 on fairway, tee, rough and cart path, 1.0 on green. So across a one-step V floor off the green a slow ball keeps 29% instead of 72%, and a swing across the floor dies in a few passes. The thud fades with speed along that axis (E6-S10 review, `floor_keep_speed` 20 yd/s off the green, `Ball.floor_thud`): `floor_keep` for a still ball, rising in proportion to speed to all of it at 20 yd/s. With `floor_keep` at every speed one tile dug a step into fairway or rough caught a 25 to 40 yd/s roll at its floor (15.0 yd from x 12.5, against 23.8 to 38.8 yd on fairway without it); now a roll at 30 yd/s or more on fairway, rough or cart path crosses such a dip within 1 yd of its run with `floor_keep` 1.0 (test), and 25 yd/s on fairway goes 22.0 yd against 23.8.
- Rest speed 0.1 yd/s. A ball slower than that stays put only when the slope's pull is weaker than the still-ball friction; otherwise it starts rolling downhill, so an uphill putt that stops short on fairway, or on a green steeper than one step per tile, rolls back. On a one-step green slope the friction outweighs the pull at every speed, so a putt slows straight to rest and stays where it runs out of pace, with no slide after it, and a ball set down there stays put (D7). A 4 yd/s putt runs about 3.7 yd on a flat green. Low points (E1-S13) are checked per axis: a ball under low point speed, 0.25 yd/s (`low_point_speed`), whose next tick would carry it from ground falling along x (or y) onto ground rising against it along that axis stops on that axis. Since the E2-S4 review the speed is checked per axis (so a ball running down a gully floor stops its slow swing across it), and a stopped axis is set exactly on the low point's line, where the slope along it is the mean of both sides. A still ball whose pull along the free axis would also cross that axis's low point rests. With both axes stopped (a pit) a still ball rests, so a ball in a one-tile pit settles at the centre instead of rocking across it until the cap. With one stopped (a gully sloping along its length) it goes on down the other, pulled by that axis's slope alone, and rests only if that pull is no more than the still-ball friction. Cup and hazards (D15), the rules E2-S6 builds. Numbers (E2-S6, starting values in `golf/ball_params.tres` and `course/surfaces.tres`):

- `cup_radius` 0.25 yd: the cup captures a ball whose centre is that close to the cup's centre, the radius included. A real cup is 0.06 yd, but at 5 yd per tile that would ask the aim for a quarter of a degree at 15 yd; 0.25 yd is a 1 degree miss at 15 yd. `cup_capture_speed` 2.0 yd/s, included: on a flat green a ball at 2 yd/s stops 1.3 yd further on, so a putt that would finish up to about 1.3 yd past drops, and a firm one rolls over. A ball under 2 yd/s moves 0.033 yd a tick, so it cannot skip the 0.5 yd disc between end-of-tick checks. A captured ball rests at the cup's centre.
- Trees `block_height` 10 yd (30 ft), on the trees row (`SurfaceRow.block_height`, 0 on every other row): a flight over a tree tile lower than 10 yd above that tile's top (its whole height, as drawn, not the bilinear ground, which leans up toward a higher neighbour) or meeting its ground is blocked, checked at the middle and end of each flight tick. A driver (apex 15 yd) is above 10 yd from about 51 to 194 yd out, so it clears a tree line in mid-flight but not near the tee or where it comes down; a wedge (apex 21 yd) clears more.
- Trees row: bounce 0, bounce friction 1.0, scatter 0, roll 0. No ball touches down or rolls on trees (a flight is blocked first, a rolling ball stops at the edge), so these only matter with `block_height` 0, where a ball would stop on the trees and be moved to the nearest open tile centre. Roll 0 takes trees out of the still-ball slope guard; `drag_full_speed` is 0.
- Scatter (`tree_scatter_min_yd` 5, `tree_scatter_max_yd` 10, `tree_scatter_cone_deg` 90, `tree_scatter_draws` 8): per draw, a distance then a turn, each `randf_range` from the ball's own generator. The reversed heading is the flight's ground velocity at the block, reversed.

- Cup: it sits at its tile's centre. It captures a ball on any surface, checked at the end of every rolling tick (a ball that rolls to rest inside the radius is captured too), never at touchdown. Touchdown speeds run far above any capture speed, so a check there would only add slam-dunks the speed rule rejects. A flight that touches down inside the radius bounces or rolls as usual and is checked on its next ground tick. E2-S6 sets the capture radius and the fastest speed that still captures.
- Water and OB entry point: the ball's ground track (its path in x and y, flight and roll alike) is walked back from where it stopped to the last point where it crossed from a tile outside the hazard into it. The hazard is water, or OB (an `oob` tile or off the map), whichever the ball stopped in. That crossing is the entry point.
- Drop point: the centre of the tile the track was over just before the entry point, if a ball may rest there. If not (water, `oob`, off the map or trees), keep walking back along the track to the first tile where it may. Never the cup's tile: the cup sits at its tile's centre, so a ball dropped there would be holed by any tap on the next stroke; the walk goes on past it as it does past trees. The same holds for a ball moved off trees to the nearest open tile centre (`Ball.may_place_on`). If there is none, it is the stroke's start. A start within `cup_radius` of the cup would leave a free tap-in, so then it is the nearest tile centre the game may place a ball on (`Ball.may_place_on`, the search a ball moved off trees uses); with none on the whole map (E2-S13), the ball goes on the cup's own tile `cup_clear_drop_yd` 2.4 yd from its centre, toward the start (`golf/ball_params.tres`; over `cup_radius`, under half a tile). So a drop never lands within `cup_radius` of the cup. The ball is placed there at rest and is not stepped again until the next stroke, so a drop on a slope cannot roll back into the hazard. One penalty stroke. The ball reports the penalty and the drop point (E2-S6) for the hole score (E2-S8).
- A ball that rolls into a tree tile stops at rest where it was on the tick before, outside the trees, as if against the trunks.
- Tree scatter (a flight blocked below E2-S6's block height): from the point where the flight was blocked, draw a distance of 1 to 2 tiles (5 to 10 yd) and a direction back toward the shot's start, within 90 degrees either way of the reversed heading, from the ball's own generator. A target on trees, water, `oob` or off the map, or within `cup_radius` of the cup (a still ball there would be holed on its next tick, E6-S13), is rejected and drawn again, up to 8 draws. Then it is the nearest tile centre a ball may rest on other than the cup's, searched ring by ring outward from the blocked point, ties in row-major order; with none on the whole map, the drop's last resort (the stroke's start, or `cup_clear_drop_yd` from the cup when the start is within `cup_radius`). A ball stopped on trees (only with `block_height` 0) moves the same way, so neither ever rests within `cup_radius` of the cup (tests in `tests/test_ball_surfaces.gd`). The ball comes down at the target with no speed and goes on as a still ball under the normal slope rules. It is deterministic by seed, so the prediction matches.
- A ball never rests on water, `oob` or inside trees: water and OB drop it, trees stop it outside or scatter it.
