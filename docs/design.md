# Prestwick: design summary for implementation

Source of intent: the Claude Docs design doc "Prestwick, Game Design Doc". This file holds the numbers and rules code depends on.

## Grid and terrain

- Tile = 5 yards. Isometric 2:1, 64x32 footprint. Height 0 to 7 steps, 16 px of screen height per step.
- Grid size (D4): 5 yards per tile everywhere, greens included. A 30 yd green is about 6x6 tiles.
- Height model (D5, D50): heights are whole steps in CourseData, drawn since E3-S58 as smooth slopes (Ground, next line), not terraces. There are no ramp tiles and no sub-step heights. A "ramp" is the slope the ball feels between two neighbouring tile centres one step apart: 2 yd over 5 yd, 40% (21.8 degrees), from the interpolated height in `golf/height_field.gd` (see Ball). E1-S10 drew an edge cue on rises that face away from the camera, which showed no riser and looked flat; since E3-S58 the slope light shows them instead. On green the ball feels a step as a 3% break, not 40% (`slope_scale`, see Ball; E1-S11), and a bank of two or more steps per tile as steep (`steep_scale`, E2-S4).
- Ground (E3-S58, D50, `course/terrain_mesh.gd` `TerrainMesh`, numbers in `course/terrain_tuning.tres`): the course view draws the land as a mesh over the bilinear surface the ball rolls on (`HeightField.height_at`), so a hill reads as a hill, not a cliff, and the picture is the surface the ball plays. A one-step rise is a slope from one tile centre to the next, across the two half tiles either side of their edge; no risers are drawn. A sloped tile (land whose 3 x 3 block of heights, clamped at the map edge, is not all one height) is a `subdiv` x `subdiv` grid (2: its vertices on the tile's edges, corners and centre lines, where the bilinear ground creases, every vertex exactly at `height_at`; 4 looked the same in screenshots and cost a fifth more to build), two triangles a grid square; a flat tile is one fan at its height, with the grid's points along each edge it shares with a sloped tile, so tiles meet without a crack. Water lies flat at its level (its tile's height); where it meets ground at another height a bank face closes the gap, and the map's two edges toward the camera show a side face down to `base_steps` 0, both in `side_color` and only where they face the camera. Texture: each tile's surface frame (labelled in Build, label-free in Play, E3-S55) maps onto its sloped ground, pulled in by 1 px so its transparent corners are never sampled: no new art. Unowned land takes `unowned_tint` and the land sheet's hatch mapped the same way. Light: every vertex is shaded `1 + light_strength * (n.x, n.y) . L`, held to `light_min` 0.55 to `light_max` 1.25, with n the ground's unit normal from its slope (`HeightField.gradient_at`'s, at a crease the mean of both sides) and L the unit `light_dir` (-1, -0.8) across the ground, from behind the hills: flat ground is 1, so a flat course draws as before; a slope turned toward the light (facing away from the camera, which the projection squeezes) is lighter and one facing the camera as much darker, so both read by value, in grayscale too (D5 finding 3). `light_strength` 0.9 (0.5 tried first: the terraces barely read at 0.175x in Build, where no contours draw). It multiplies the height shade (next line), under the frame's headroom cap. Draw order: a ray to the camera only ever moves to larger i + j, so painting depth rows back to front is exact for a height field; the mesh is cut into chunks of one depth row and `chunk_tiles` 32 tiles along it, each Y-sorted at its row's `Iso.depth_key`, and every object on a tile (`Iso.object_sort_key`, the row's key + 1.5 and more) draws over its row and under the rows in front, so a hill in front hides what stands behind it. Built once per course (`CourseView.show_course`); an edit re-emits the tiles within 2 of each changed tile and rejoins their chunks (`refresh_cells`), a purchase its parcel's tiles (`refresh_parcel`). Picking (`course/tile_picker.gd`) reads exactly what is drawn (`TerrainMesh.ground_in`): the ray is walked from one mesh grid line to the next, between which it stays over one flat triangle, and the first tile whose ground (or face) it meets is picked; the aim cursor in Play aims at that ground point. Measured on the playtest nine (`scripts/terrain_perf.gd`, 1280 x 720, vsync off, map centre): drawing the whole course 379 ms once (terraces: 106), the redraw after a one-tile raise 1.8 ms and after a 5 x 5 raise 5.3 ms (terraces: 0.06 and 0.45); idle frames in Build 2.6 ms at 0.175x, 1.3 at 0.5x, 0.9 at 1x (terraces: 5.0, 2.8, 1.8), in Play with the contours hidden 2.8, 1.3, 0.9 (5.8, 2.6, 1.7) and shown 4.5, 2.1, 1.3 (8.1, 3.5, 2.4): 32-tile chunks are fewer draw items than the tile map's quadrants (8-tile chunks measured 6.3 ms at 0.175x). Screenshots `docs/decisions/e3-s58-*.png`.
- Height shade (E6-S18, `art/specs/surfaces.md`): the top of each land surface, rough, water, trees and oob (`height_shaded` in `course/builder_tuning.tres`), is multiplied by a grey value for its height, `height_shade` (0.80, 0.90, 1.00, 1.025, 1.05, 1.07, 1.09, 1.10 for heights 0 to 7; top brought down from 1.15 in E6-S28), so low ground draws darker and high ground lighter, by value (colorblind rule). Course surfaces (fairway, green, tee, bunker, cart path) take no shade, so at 0.5x, where labels and stipple are gone, their colour still names the lie and the target (pillar 2); their slopes' light shows their relief (E3-S58; before it their edge cues and risers did). Since E3-S58 the shade is read at the ground's own height, blended between the whole steps' values. The ramp is narrow so rough's value band over heights 0 to 7 stays at least 5% clear of fairway, tee, green and trees (E6-S18 review; `tests/test_course_view.gd`), and so every shaded land surface at every height stays at least 5% in value from fairway, tee and green at the plot's height, measured both as the frame's mean luminance and as its fill's (its most common colour: at 0.5x rough's speckle is gone and only the fill tells it from tee; E6-S28). The tee's fill (139 of 255) is the tight one, so rough's fill (119) may rise at most to about 1.12; 1.15 put rough at height 7 within 2% of the tee. Bunker sand, the lightest surface, is unshaded and keeps its tint at height 7 (and the headroom cap would hold it short of white if it were ever shaded; E6-S28). Above 1 a frame's shade is capped where its brightest coloured pixel would reach 255 (`CourseView.frame_headroom`), so no channel clips and the hue holds; water, whose light ramp is 232, tops out at about 1.10. Only the near-grey label chalk may clip toward white. A height-6 plateau reads from a height-2 lowland, and across a rise that faces away from the camera, where only the edge cue's lip shows, the lighter side is the higher one, so the slope direction reads too (generated land, E6-S2; land prices follow relief, E6-S8). The land hatch keeps its own values; on unowned land the ground's shade is its height shade times `unowned_tint` (risers and edge cues, which kept their own values, are no longer drawn since E3-S58). The entry at `plot_ground` (2) stays 1, so a New sandbox draws as before. It was a modulate on per-height alternative tiles (`CourseView.top_alt`) until E3-S58 made it a vertex colour on the ground mesh, not new frames: a whole 128 x 128 draw went from about 216 to 235 ms (headless, debug), within D10's budget. Value here is Rec. 709 luminance (Godot's `Color.get_luminance`, 0 to 255 in these figures): the placeholder fills are fairway 153, tee 139 and rough 119; other brightness measures give other numbers (the E6-S28 review's 139 and 137.5 for fairway and tee match none tried), so quote this one. Screenshots `docs/decisions/e6-s18-*.png` (`tests/fixtures/land_shot.tscn`, seed 7).
- Tile labels (E3-S55, `art/specs/surfaces.md`): the placeholder surface (and riser, no longer drawn since E3-S58) frames carry a two-letter label (FW, RO, RB, ...) for Build, where the palette and the map name each surface while it is painted. Play draws label-free copies of the same sheets (`scripts/placeholder.py` writes `surfaces_play_placeholder.png` and `risers_play_placeholder.png`, the same frames and patterns with the pattern running under where the label was), so the course reads as a course and no white specks show at 0.5x. `TileSheet.play_texture` names a sheet's Play copy, and `CourseView.play_art` (Main sets it with the mode) swaps the ground chunks' textures, with no rebuild of the mesh. The Build palette's icons keep their labels. A sheet with no Play copy (final art, which has no labels; the edge cue and land sheets) draws the same in both modes. Screenshots: `docs/decisions/e3-s55-play-0.5x.png`, `e3-s55-play-1x.png`, `e3-s55-build-1x.png` (`tests/fixtures/tile_labels_shot_*.tscn`).
- Contours in Play (E3-S57, `course/topo_overlay.gd` `TopoOverlay`, numbers in `course/topo_tuning.tres`): Play draws topographic contour lines over the visible land, traced from `HeightField.height_at` by `ContourTracer` (E3-S56, `subdiv` 2), a line every `interval` 1 step from `offset` 0.5 (0 until E3-S59), in ink (`line_width_px` 2 at every zoom, `line_alpha` 0.8) with short downhill ticks on each line's low side (`tick_length_px` 5 every `tick_spacing_px` 36). Since E3-S59 (D50) each line is drawn at its own height, which is the drawn ground's (the course view draws the bilinear ground the tracer follows), so it lies on the slope it marks, unbroken, and each tick's foot runs downhill on the ground; no piece is drawn over water, which lies flat at its level. Offset 0.5 puts each line half way up every slope, where the shaded slope is (`docs/decisions/e3-s59-contours-1x.png`); offset 0, which outlined each terrace through its edge tiles' centres while the ground was drawn as terraces (E3-S57: `placement` GROUND, D17's tile-top rule, and the half-step options set aside then), now draws along the top edge of each slope (`e3-s59-option-contours-whole-1x.png`); `placement` is gone. Each line is cut at tile edges and Y-sorts with the terrain at its tile's row key + 0.25 (`TopoOverlay.SORT_BIAS`; its tile's top key + 0.25 before E3-S59), so a hill in front hides it and the ball, golfers, flags, tags and aim draw over it. It traces when the course changes or the view leaves the traced window (the visible tiles grown by `margin_tiles` 24) and lays out again only on a zoom step; never per frame. `toggle_topo` (T) hides and shows it, on by default in Play, the choice held; never drawn in Build. Measured on the playtest nine (`scripts/topo_perf.gd`, 1280 x 720, vsync off): entering Play at 0.175x traces the whole map and lays it out once, about 87 ms (trace 51, layout 30; a zoom step's layout about 30 ms); idle frames at 0.175x 7.8 ms mean, 9.4 p95, against 5.6 and 6.5 with it hidden; at 0.5x 3.4 against 2.5. Since E3-S59 (one row per depth row: 210 rows on the playtest nine, against 671): entering Play 84 ms (trace 49, layout 23), idle at 0.175x 3.7 ms against 2.8 hidden, at 0.5x 2.0 against 1.2 (with the smooth ground's own frames, E3-S58). Screenshots `docs/decisions/e3-s57-0.175x.png`, `-0.5x.png`, `-0.5x-gray.png`, `-1x.png`, `-2x.png`, and the options set aside `-option-level-half-1x.png`, `-option-ground-half-1x.png`.
- Projection (`course/iso.gd`, the only one): tile (i, j) at height h has its center at screen `x = (i - j) * 32`, `y = (i + j) * 16 - h * 16`. Draw order is back to front by i + j (the ground's depth rows, E3-S58), then whatever stands on each row.
- Camera: fixed isometric, with one overhead inset: on the green, Play opens a top-down green view over it (D48, E3-S52, Swing: Green view). Zoom steps 0.175x, 0.5x, 1x, 2x (D45: the farthest added by E3-S9, so a long hole frames with its tee and cup inside the free view, and the zoom keys reach it too); WASD pan 600 screen px/s at every zoom; middle-mouse drag; center may go 128 px past the course edge. Tuned in `scenes/camera_tuning.tres`. The farthest step is sized from `tests/fixtures/nine_holes.gd`'s longest hole at 1280 x 720: hole 3, a 520 yd par 5 along a tile axis, is 3328 x 1680 px at 1x against a free view of 731 x 622 px, and needs 0.181x with `aim_frame_margin` each side; 0.18x would leave 4 px of slack, so 0.175x (21 px), a 128 x 128 map then about 1434 px across. At 0.175x a tile is 11 x 6 screen px and a height step 2.8 px: terrain still reads by surface colour, water, trees and risers (the rough's stipple turns to a fine speckle); the guests (24 px, `figure_min_screen_scale`), the ball (`ball_min_screen_radius`), the aim line, cues, tags and issue boxes keep their screen size as at every zoom; tee markers and pins would shrink to a few px and be cut by the tiles in front, so below 0.5x they are drawn at their 0.5x size on screen (`marker_min_screen_scale` 0.5, `course/builder_tuning.tres`), over the terrain (z_index 1), and at 0.5x and nearer at their own size, Y-sorted as before (`HoleMarker.shape_scale`); the player's ball and the guests go to z_index 1 at the same zooms (`CourseView.lifted_z`), so they still Y-sort with the grown markers: a ball on its tee draws over the tee's blocks and number, a guest in front of a pin over the pin. Measured with `scripts/nine_holes_perf.gd` (nine holes, real display, 1280 x 720, vsync off, other jobs on the machine so the figures are noisy, 2026-11-05): idle Build frames with the script's guest day at 3x run median 1.2 to 1.3 ms at 1x, 1.9 to 2.9 at 0.5x and 5.8 to 8.8 at 0.175x (p95 at 0.175x 13.7 to 18.3, worst 17.4 to 23.4); in Play, with the main scene's own day at 3x stepped to 80 s so a full field is drawn (14 to 16 figures), median 7.7 ms at 0.175x and 3.8 at 0.5x (p95 15.1 and 11.9, worst 19.8 and 14.2). Frames at 0.175x go over 16.7 ms: p95 about 15 to 18 and the worst 19 to 27 ms, where at 0.5x they stay under it. The draw causes it: the whole map costs 2 to 2.5 times what 0.5x does (no guests: median 6.2 against 3.4 ms in Build), and on top of that the 3x guest step's peaks (E3-S14), which fit at 0.5x, go past a frame. Cutting the draw cost at the farthest step is a follow-up. Screenshots `docs/decisions/e3-s9-frame-longest.png`, `docs/decisions/e3-s9-play-0.175x.png`.
- Play camera (E2-S8, `scenes/course_camera.gd`): in Play the camera centres on the ball when Play starts (on the tee), when a shot starts, while the ball moves, and after it rests or is dropped. It eases onto the ball with a time constant of `centre_time` 0.15 s in `scenes/camera_tuning.tres` (95% of the way in 0.45 s; 0 jumps), so a moving ball stays centred without a jolt at each stroke. A pan (WASD or middle drag) stops the centring so the player can scout; `snap_camera` (C) snaps back to the ball and centres on it again. Zoom does not stop it. A shot starting centres on the ball even after a pan. While the player aims at rest (E2-S8 review, pillar 2), the camera frames the ball and the aim marker together: it centres on their midpoint, held so the ball stays at least `aim_frame_margin` 64 screen px inside the edge, and when both do not fit it steps the zoom out (never past `aim_farthest_zoom` 0.5x, `scenes/camera_tuning.tres`; D45 keeps the aim off the farthest step, which only the zoom keys and hole framing reach) until they do. On `courses/example_par3.json` the driver's marker from the tee is (1568, 784) px away at 1x, so the tee opens at 0.5x; the 6-iron's (1005, 502) fits at 1x. A flight follows the ball alone at the zoom the aim chose, and the next aim (Play or the ball at rest) starts again from the player's own zoom, the level of their last zoom key. A zoom key wins over the fit until the next aim, and a panned camera never zooms. The swing panel hint names WASD (pan) and C (back to ball).
- Brush sizes 1, 3, 5: an n x n square centered on the cursor tile, clipped to the map. Sizes and the undo depth (200 steps) are tuned in `course/builder_tuning.tres`.
- Plot (D10): a new sandbox (E2-S3) is 128 x 128 tiles (640 yd along an edge, 905 yd corner to corner, about 85 acres), every tile rough at height 2. A par 5 needs more than 94 tiles of straight line (470 / 5), so a 520 yd par 5 (104 tiles) fits along an edge with 24 tiles to spare, with room for water on its line. Height 2 lets the builder dig two steps for water and bunkers and raise five. The plot's cost is E4-S1's. - Land (D19): a new course is generated natural land (hills, tree stands, ponds, rough), split into parcels. The player starts owning a small block and buys parcels that border owned land from a land budget; building and holes go only on owned land. New sandbox stays as a separate mode on the D10 plot with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget. Settled by E6-S1 (D26 to D30): the numbers and rules are in Land, below.
- Out of bounds (D9, D29): OB is off the map, every `oob` tile, and, outside New sandbox, every tile on a parcel the player does not own. From E2-S3 `oob` is on the Build palette as OB ground, and course files already carry it (surface 8). Placed OB stakes stay in the Icebox. A ball that comes to rest OB costs one stroke and is dropped (Ball, Cup and hazards).
- Builder (`course/builder.gd`, the only terrain writer with `course/save.gd`): paint any of the 9 surfaces below (`oob` from E2-S3, D9), raise or lower one step per click, heights clamp at 0 and 7. One click, or one drag, is one undo step; a drag raises or lowers each tile once. Edits that change nothing are not recorded. Ctrl+Z (`ui_undo`) undoes. Active in Build mode only.
- Hole list (E3-S2): a course holds up to `max_holes` holes (9, `course/builder_tuning.tres`), numbered 1 to 9. A tee or cup for a number with no hole creates it; New hole in the hole panel picks the lowest free number (`CourseBuilder.next_hole_number`), so holes are numbered in build order, and the Tee tool. A tenth hole, or a number outside 1 to 9, is refused ("a course has at most 9 holes"). The list above the hole panel has one row per hole in number order: up and down give the hole the number above or below, swapping with the hole there (`renumber_hole`); the row's button picks the hole for the Tee and Cup tools (`BuildTool.select_hole`) and shows its par, yardage and status; Open or Close opens or closes it in one click (`close_hole`); Play plays it. Renumber and Close are undo steps, keep every open flag and the course's hole order, and `course/save.gd` writes the numbers as they are. Play and Tab play the picked hole (`SwingControl.hole_to_play`; a picked hole with no tee yet plays the lowest-numbered hole that has one). ## Land

Settled on paper by E6-S1 (D26 to D30, user 2026-11-03), built in E6-S2 to E6-S8. New sandbox (D10) is unchanged by all of it: flat rough, every parcel owned, an unlimited budget, buying hidden.

- Parcels (D26): 16 x 16 tiles, 80 yd on a side, 6,400 sq yd (about 1.3 acres). Parcel (p, q) holds tiles x `16p` to `16p + 15` and y `16q` to `16q + 15`, clipped to the map, so a map of w x h tiles has `ceil(w / 16)` x `ceil(h / 16)` parcels and only a map whose size is not a multiple of 16 has partial ones. The 128 x 128 map (D10's size, so D10's draw time holds; frames on generated land run about 2.2 ms at 0.5x and 1.5 ms at 1x against D10's flat 1.0 and 0.7 ms, Plot above) is 8 x 8 = 64 parcels. The parcel size is a course format constant, `CourseData.PARCEL_TILES` 16, not a tunable in `data/land.json`: format v2's owned flags are indexed by parcel, so a different size would read every saved file against the wrong grid. Changing it means a new format version with a `course/save.gd` migration.
- Ownership (D26) is part of CourseData, the single source of truth: one owned flag per parcel. Next to the flags CourseData holds the land budget left: a whole number of dollars, or unlimited (`null` in the file) for New sandbox, which is how a saved file tells a sandbox from a New course. Only `course/builder.gd` (a New course or New sandbox, a purchase, and its undo) and `course/save.gd` (a load) write either. Course format version 2 saves both, so a New course saved after two purchases reloads with what was left, not a fresh $5,000; `course/save.gd` loads a version 1 file with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget, the same as New sandbox, so every course and fixture made before E6 plays as before (E6-S3). Built in E6-S3: `CourseData` reads `parcels_wide`, `parcels_high`, `parcel_of`, `parcel_rect`, `is_parcel_owned`, `is_owned(x, y)` (off-map tiles are never owned), `land_budget` and `has_unlimited_budget()`; `CourseData.create` (New sandbox, fixtures) owns every parcel with no limit. In the file, `owned` is one string of `"0"` and `"1"` per parcel row and `land_budget` the dollars left or `null` (`.claude/skills/course-file/SKILL.md`); `CourseSave.migrate` turns a version 1 file into that. The builder buys through `buy_parcel` and says why not through `buy_refusal`; prices come from `course/land_prices.gd` (`LandPrices`, reading `data/land.json`).
- Starting block (D26): 2 x 2 parcels at the centre of the map, owned for free when a New course starts: parcels 3 and 4 on each axis of the 8 x 8, tiles 48 to 79 on both axes, 32 x 32 tiles, 160 yd on a side. The camera already frames the map's centre (Main), so the game opens on it.
- New course (E6-S6): `CourseBuilder.new_course(seed)` makes the generated land of that seed (`LandGenerator.generate`), owns the starting block and nothing else, and sets the land budget to `land_budget` in `data/land.json` ($5,000); no holes. The same seed gives the same course. The main scene starts on one (`Main.course_path` empty; a failed load warns and does the same), with the seed from `Main.start_seed` (-1, the default, picks one at random; `Main.land_seed` says which), and the camera frames the map's centre, where the block is, at 1x (`docs/decisions/e6-s6-new-course.png`). The Build HUD has New course (fresh land from a random seed) above New sandbox; each hands the course to a fresh builder (empty undo stack). New sandbox stays D10's flat rough plot with every parcel owned and an unlimited budget, and the Build palette hides Buy land on any course with an unlimited budget (New sandbox and every version 1 file, which own every parcel); a selected Buy land steps back to painting. The land panel still shows "Land budget: unlimited" there. Tests: `tests/test_new_course.gd`.
- Buying (D27): a parcel that is not owned may be bought when it shares an edge with an owned parcel (a shared corner only is not enough) and its price is no more than the land budget left. A refusal names the rule that failed: "not next to your land" or "costs $X, you have $Y" (money written as "$1,150"); a parcel already owned gives "already yours" and one off the map "no such parcel". With an unlimited budget only the edge rule applies and a purchase costs nothing. A purchase is one undo step on the builder's stack (E6-S3); undoing it gives the price back and the parcel up, and reruns the validator, so a hole that now stands on unowned land closes. Land is never sold back: there is no sell action, and undo is the only way a parcel is given up.
- Land budget (D28): $5,000 (`land_budget` in `data/land.json`) when a New course starts, spent on parcels only until E4-S1 folds it into the one balance (Economy and time). The starting block is free and does not come out of it. New sandbox has no budget limit.
- Parcel price (D28), kept as E6-S1 set it by E6-S8's `/balance land` (below). Read from the parcel's tiles: they are the generated land, since unowned land cannot be edited (E6-S4), so a parcel's price never changes before it is bought. All numbers in `data/land.json`:

  `price = base_price + relief_step_price × relief + tree_tile_price × trees + water_tile_price × water`, clamped to `min_price` to `max_price` and rounded to the nearest `price_round`.

  | Term | Value | Read as |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `base_price` | $1,000 | every parcel |
  | `relief_step_price` | +$100 per step | relief: the parcel's highest tile height minus its lowest (0 to 7), so hills cost more |
  | `tree_tile_price` | -$1 per tile | tree tiles on the parcel (0 to 256), ground to clear before a hole goes there |
  | `water_tile_price` | -$2 per tile | water tiles on the parcel, ground a hole plays round |
  | `min_price`, `max_price` | $500, $2,000 | clamp |
  | `price_round` | $50 | |

  Worked prices (hand calculation): flat open rough (relief 0, no trees or water) $1,000. A typical generated parcel at the rates below (relief 2, 31 trees, 10 water) is 1000 + 200 - 31 - 20 = 1149, $1,150. A hill parcel (relief 5, 20 trees) 1000 + 500 - 20 = 1480, $1,500. A wooded valley (relief 1, 80 trees, 20 water) 1000 + 100 - 80 - 40 = 980, $1,000. So $5,000 buys 4 typical parcels ($4,600) and leaves $400. Range: the generator's rough floor (at least 60% of a parcel, so at most 102 tree or water tiles) puts every generated parcel between relief 0 with 102 water, 1000 - 204 = 796, $800, and relief 7 with none, $1,700. So the $500 and $2,000 clamp only guards tuned values and partial parcels, and trees and water take at most about $100 to $200 off while relief adds up to $700: at these provisional numbers a wooded or wet parcel is only slightly cheaper. E6-S8's `/balance land` decides whether that is enough, and if bargain parcels should exist, raises the tree and water discounts rather than loosening the rough floor.

  Measured (E6-S8, `/balance land`, seeds 1 to 200): unowned parcels cost $900 to $1,600, median $1,050, 10th to 90th percentile $950 to $1,300, and none reaches the clamp; relief is mostly 0 to 3, so the typical parcel is cheaper than the hand calculation's $1,150. Trees and water take $52 off a parcel on average and $204 at most. The 8 parcels next to the block differ by $354 on average from dearest to cheapest, mostly by relief. Spent cheapest buyable parcel first, $5,000 buys 4 to 5 parcels (4.98 on average, $278 left), so a New course owns 8 or 9 parcels. Every target in Balance targets, land holds, so no number in `data/land.json` moved. The discounts stay small on purpose: until E4 painting is free, so clearing a tree or filling a pond costs nothing, and a bigger discount would pay the player to buy woods and clear them for free. E4-S1, which gives paint a cost, is where a wooded or wet parcel's discount should be set against what clearing it costs (Economy and time).
- A ball on unowned land (D29): out of bounds, as an `oob` tile is (D9, D15): a ball that touches down on or rolls onto a tile of an unowned parcel stops there, takes one penalty stroke and is dropped behind its entry point, and the drop walk passes over unowned tiles as it does over `oob` (`Ball.may_place_on` is false there). A flight over unowned land that comes down on owned land plays on. The user's answer was that a ball which comes to rest on unowned land is OB; measuring it as an `oob` tile (a ball that rolls onto unowned land is OB even if it would have rolled back) is the implementer's reading, chosen because existing OB tiles stop the ball that way, and E6-S8's hand playtest checks it (D29). `ShotPrediction` reads the same ownership, so the aim marker shows the OB outcome before the shot (pillar 2). The line between owned and unowned land is drawn as a boundary in Play and Build (E6-S5, E6-S7). Unowned land keeps its real heights, which the ball's height field still reads (a hill on unowned land still feeds a slope beside it). Built in E6-S7: `HeightField.surface_at` (and `surface_of(tile)`) reads every tile of an unowned parcel as `oob`, whatever is painted there, so the ball, `Ball.may_rest_on` and `may_place_on`, the drop walk, tree scatter, the lie and `ShotPrediction` all see the line through that one read, and a fully owned course (New sandbox, every version 1 file) reads every tile's own surface, so its play is unchanged tick for tick. Trees on unowned land still block a flight (E6-S7 review): the in-flight tree test (`Ball._blocks`, so the scatter, `ShotPrediction.stop_point` and the D20 blocked check) reads the painted surface (`HeightField.painted_surface_at`), because the trees are drawn there and trees past the stakes stop a real ball; touchdown, roll, rest and the drop still read unowned land as `oob`, and the scatter rests only on owned land. The land line in Play (E6-S7): `golf/land_boundary.gd` (`LandBoundary.segments`) lists every tile edge between an owned tile and an unowned one, walked parcel by parcel (the map's own edge is not one), on the drawn ground, the higher side of the two at a water bank, in halves of a tile edge (E3-S59; on the higher tile top of the two before); `scenes/aim_view.gd` draws it with the aim, before every shot, as chalk dashes on a solid ink line (white stakes: a pattern as well as a shade, and dashes, not the aim's dots), 2 px wide with 6 px dashes at 1x (`land_line_width`, `land_line_dash`, exported on AimView beside its other drawing sizes). AimView draws over the terrain, so an edge that a tile in front covers (`LandBoundary.covered`: a nearer tile's column crosses the line of sight to the edge's middle) draws faded (`land_line_hidden_alpha` 0.3), as an issue marker's leader does behind a hill (E2-S17), and the line does not read as running over the hill's top. The line stays drawn through the struck ball's flight and its water or OB hold (E6-S16): the strike calls `AimView.clear_aim`, which drops the dots and marker and keeps the line, so the player sees the boundary the ball crossed; Build and a new course clear it. The aim marker shows the OB outcome: when the prediction ends in water or OB (`penalty_strokes`), it draws the cue the struck ball shows there (ShotView, `scenes/hazard_cue.gd`: a flag_red circle struck through by a slash running past it for OB, chalk splash rings for water) at `ShotPrediction.hazard_position`, where the dots end; a putt draws it in place of the rest ring, and a full club in place of its landing ring when it lands in the hazard, or beside the ring when it lands clear and rolls in. - Hand calculation: the starting block fits a par 3, not a par 4 (D26, D30). In tiles, the block's tile centres run 0 to 31 on each axis. Under D30 the tee and cup may be any two owned tiles, so the longest hole is corner to corner: `5 × sqrt(31² + 31²) = 5 × 43.84 = 219.2` yd, rounded 219, a par 3 (rule 4: up to 319 since D33). A par 4 needs 320 yd (250 before D33), more than the block's 219, so no par 4 fits, whatever the land. A 160 yd par 3 (the `/course-file generate 3` hole) fits: tee at block tile (0, 12) and cup at (31, 20) is `5 × sqrt(31² + 8²) = 160.08` yd, 160, both owned, with its rule-3 path along the line on owned land; or across the diagonal, tee (4, 4) to cup (27, 27), `5 × sqrt(23² + 23²) = 162.6` yd, 163, with 20 yd of owned land behind the tee and past the cup. Its footprint then reaches unowned land past the tee and the cup and, on the first layout, beside them, which D30 allows (one shot there is OB). Had D30 asked for the whole footprint, the tee and the cup would both have to sit on tiles 6 to 25 of each axis (a tile at index 5 is 6 tiles, 30 yd, from the unowned tiles at -1), and the longest hole would be `5 × sqrt(19² + 19²) = 134.4` yd: no 160 yd par 3, and a par 4 only on 2 x 4 parcels (`5 × sqrt(19² + 51²) = 272` yd), 4 purchases, against 1 under D30. One purchase beside the block (5 parcels, a 32 x 48 tile bounding box) gives `5 × sqrt(31² + 47²) = 281.5` yd: room for a par 4 of 250 to 281 yd before D33, but no par 4 since D33 raised the shortest to 320 yd. Two purchases in a line off the block (6 parcels, a 32 x 64 tile bounding box) give `5 × sqrt(31² + 63²) = 351.1` yd straight across the corners (the E6-S8 playtest's par 4 is 320 yd from a tee 2 tiles in and 3 tiles off the side to a cup 3 tiles off the far side), so since D33 the second hole comes with the second purchase (D34 records this against D26's Why and D30's Benefit); the land target below holds it on every seed. Nine holes need about 34 parcels of footprint alone (a capsule of `(L + 60) × 60` sq yd per hole: a 160 yd par 3 2.1 parcels, a 380 yd par 4 4.1, a 520 yd par 5 5.4; three par 3s, four par 4s and two par 5s), far more than the block and the 4 parcels $5,000 buys, so E4's money has work to do (E6-S8's target).
- Generated land (E6-S2, rates set by E6-S1), on the 128 x 128 map from an integer seed: the same seed gives the same map. It uses only rough, trees and water, and heights 0 to 7; fairway, green, tee, bunker, cart path and `oob` are the player's to build. Rates, each a target E6-S2's test holds within its tolerance over seeds 1 to 20, tunables in `course/land_tuning.tres`:

  | What | Target | Tolerance |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Rough share of the map | the rest, about 84% | |
  | Trees share | 12%, in 15 to 35 stands of 20 to 200 tiles | 8% to 16% |
  | Water share | 4%, in 4 to 10 ponds of 15 to 120 tiles | 2% to 6% |
  | Base height (D10's lowland) | 2 | |
  | Hills | 4 to 8 per map, peaks 2 to 5 steps above the base (so up to 7) | heights 0 to 7 always |
  | Tiles at height 4 or more | 15% | 8% to 25% |
  | Steepest generated slope | one step between edge neighbours, at most one riser every 2 tiles along either axis | exact |
  | Ponds | flat, one step below their lowest shore tile, never below 0 | exact |
  | Every parcel | at least 60% of its tiles rough, and never all water | exact |
  | Starting block | at least 85% rough and no water, so the first par 3 needs no pond | exact |

  Why one riser every 2 tiles at most: long rough slopes of one step per tile settle in up to 14.8 s, over D16's 10 s off-green target (Ball, Known limits, E6-S10), while slopes stepping every 2 tiles settle under 7 s on rough. So generated land meets D16 before E6-S10, and steeper ground is the player's own doing. Since E6-S20 `/course-file generate hilly` lays its hole over this land (D19; Holes, Generated courses).

  How it is built (E6-S2, `course/land_generator.gd`, `LandGenerator.generate(seed)`, about 45 ms on 128 x 128): one `RandomNumberGenerator` seeded with the seed, three passes in order. It never writes a CourseData: it builds the tiles and loads them through `CourseData.from_dict`, as `CourseGenerator` does.
  - Hills: `hill_count_min` to `hill_count_max` (4 to 8) hills at random centres, each a cosine bell `peak` steps high (`hill_peak_min` to `hill_peak_max`, 2 to 5) and `peak × hill_radius_per_step` tiles wide (5 to 8 tiles per step), plus 0 to `hill_lobes_max` (2) smaller lobes `hill_lobe_offset` (0.45 to 0.85) of its radius off its centre at `hill_lobe_scale` (0.45 to 0.8) of its peak and radius, so hills are not round. The bells are summed by their highest, rounded to whole steps on 2 x 2 tile terrace cells, and clamped so neighbouring cells differ by one step at most: a riser can only fall on a cell edge, which is what makes the slope rule exact. Every radius is then scaled by one factor between `hill_scale_min` and `hill_scale_max` (0.6 to 1.6), found by bisection, so that `high_ground_share` (15%) of the map is at `high_ground_level` (4) or more; on seeds 1 to 20 it lands on 15.0%.
  - Ponds: 6 to 10 ponds (`ponds_min` to `ponds_max`, raised to at least `water_share` over `pond_max_tiles`), each sized to share what is left of the `water_share` target (±30%, within `pond_min_tiles` to `pond_max_tiles`). A pond grows by 2 x 2 squares from a random square, never leaving a one-tile gap along an axis, on rough ground that is flat for 2 tiles all round and at height 1 or more, outside the starting block, and is then dug one step. A pond that cannot reach `pond_min_tiles` is dropped and tried elsewhere.
  - Tree stands: 15 to 35 stands (`tree_stands_min` to `tree_stands_max`, kept where `tree_share` over the stand sizes allows), each sized to share what is left of the `tree_share` target (±40%, within `tree_stand_min_tiles` to `tree_stand_max_tiles`), grown on rough one random neighbouring tile at a time, never touching another stand, so each stand is one patch.
  - Floors: a pond square or a tree tile that would take a parcel under `parcel_min_rough` (60%) rough, or the starting block under `start_block_min_rough` (85%), is not laid.
  - Every number above is in `course/land_tuning.tres`; `tests/test_land_generator.gd` holds the table's targets over seeds 1 to 20 (and held them over 1 to 150 when written). The 2 x 2 terrace cell and the 2-tile flat margin round a pond are structural (`LandGenerator.TERRACE`, `POND_MARGIN`), not tunables: they are what the slope and pond rules rest on. `tests/fixtures/land_shot.tscn` shows a seed in Build (`LAND_SEED`, `LAND_ZOOM`, `LAND_FOCUS`).

## Hole validation rules (each is a GUT test)

1. Exactly one tee and one cup.
2. Cup sits on a green tile.
3. A path of non-water, non-OB tiles connects tee to cup.
4. Yardage along the line of play matches par (D47: the straight tee-to-cup line, or tee to turn to cup on a dogleg): par 3 < 320, par 4 320 to 470, par 5 > 470 (the shortest par 4 was 250 until D33). A par 3 of about 298 to 319 yd cannot be reached in one on level ground (its green front is past a perfect drive's 282 yd total). It stays a par 3 (no capped band, which would leave 298 to 319 yd holes with no par, against pillar 1); instead rule 5 gives every hole of 300 yd or more a landing zone whatever its par (D35, E6-S37). Yardage stays flat, with no allowance for the height between tee and cup: on generated land 9 of 320 perfect drives (2.8%, `/balance clubs` since E6-S34's bounce budget; 14% before it) reach the shortest par 4's green down falling ground. Measured before E6-S34, a tee-to-cup allowance only took the 14% to about 11% at any rate per step, because the extra length comes from the ground where the ball lands and rolls, not from the cup's height (D35, E6-S25). A course saved before D33 with an open par 4 of 250 to 319 yd loads with it closed on this rule until it is set to par 3; a load (E4-S7) or import (E5-S5) shows one notice listing every hole the validator closed with its one-line reason, for example "Hole 3 closed: 280 yd is a par 3, not par 4" (D35). A course saved before D47 with an open dogleg whose line of play changes its par loads the same way, for example "Hole 3 closed: 512 yd round the dogleg is a par 5, not par 4" (`CourseBuilder.closed_on_load` holds those lines).
5. No tee inside another hole's landing zone.
6. Tee and cup on owned land (D30, E6-S4; New sandbox owns every parcel, so it always passes there).

How each rule is measured (D11). Distances are between tile centres at 5 yd per tile. `course/validator.gd` (E2-S2) is the one implementation, called by the game and by `/course-file validate` (`course/course_file.gd`). Each failing rule returns the tile at fault and a one-line reason.

- Rule 1, one tee and one cup (E2-S2): a hole fails when its tee or cup is not placed yet (off the map, `HoleData.UNPLACED`; the other rules are then not measured), when its tee and cup are the same tile, or when another hole has the same number (a second tee and cup for that hole; each stands on its own tee and names the other by its label, "shares number 4 with hole 4b", the same words on the map, the hole list and `/course-file`, which heads each hole by its label too: E3-S41). A placed tee or cup is the tile at fault.

- Rule 2, cup on green: the cup's tile is the tile at fault.
- Rule 3, path: tiles connect through their 4 edge neighbours (no diagonals). Every in-map surface except water and `oob` may be crossed, trees included. Heights are ignored. A tile on an unowned parcel counts as `oob` (D30, E6-S4). The tee tile and the cup tile are part of the path. The tile at fault is the first water or `oob` tile on a 4-connected walk along the tee-to-cup line (E2-S2); a walk with none would itself be a path, so there always is one.
- Rule 4, yardage (D47, E3-S25): the line of play, rounded to the nearest whole yard (`roundi`, `CourseValidator.hole_yardage`). On a straight hole that is the straight line from tee centre to cup centre, `5 * sqrt(dx² + dy²)` yd (`CourseValidator.yardage`); on a dogleg it is tee centre to the turn plus the turn to cup centre. Every hole of 240 yd or more on the straight line (`landing_distance_yd`; a shorter one is in reach of a tee shot and played at its cup) has rule 4's disc: 30 yd (`zone_radius_yd`) round the point `min(240 yd, yardage - 60 yd)` from the tee on the straight line, which is rule 5's zone on a hole of 300 yd or more (E3-S25 review: rule 4 does not wait for `zone_from_yd`, so a sharp dogleg under 300 yd straight is measured round its corner too). A hole is a dogleg when no fairway tile's centre lies inside that disc; its tee shot is then played to its own fairway instead. The turn (`CourseValidator.turn_point`) is the centre of that fairway at the disc's distance from the tee: of the fairway tiles whose centres are that far from the tee, to half a tile, and ahead of the tee (on the cup's side of the line through the tee perpendicular to the tee-to-cup line), the 8-neighbour runs whose fairway runs on, 8 neighbours through fairway, to a tile centre within 40 yd of the cup (`turn_cup_reach_yd`, `course/validator_tuning.tres`: the fairway that leads to this hole's green); of those, the run nearest the disc's centre, and the mean of its tile centres. With no such run the hole stays straight, so another hole's fairway beside or behind the tee, or across a forced carry's zone, never bends a hole, and painting a neighbour's fairway never closes an open hole (E3-S25 review: before the fairway had to lead to the cup and lie ahead, a 400 yd hole with no fairway of its own beside another hole's measured 575 to 852 yd). So a hole with no fairway, a straight fairway through its disc, a cross bunker on the disc's centre with fairway round it, a dogleg whose corner fairway stops more than 40 yd short of the cup, and every hole under 240 yd straight keep the straight number, and the boundary cases below are unchanged. A hairpin under 240 yd straight is measured straight (a limit of the 240 yd threshold, put to the user in E3-S25's review). The yardage follows the fairway (D11's cost of a line of play): painting the corner moves the turn, and painting it rough straightens the hole; the rerun after every edit closes an open hole whose par it no longer matches. A par 4 or 5 on the straight line always has a rule 5 zone; a dogleg under 300 yd straight that rule 4 makes a par 4 has none (rule 5 reads the straight line). Rule 5's zone, its threshold, the footprint, guests' aim and the fair check keep the straight line (D47). The rounded number is the one the card, the hole list and `/course-file validate` and `inspect` show, and par is checked on it: par 3 up to 319, par 4 320 to 470 (both included), par 5 471 and up (D33, E6-S19; par 4 from 250 before, which a perfect drive reached, below). The raw straight value is never exactly x.5 (`sqrt(n) = (2k + 1) / 10` has no whole n), so rounding has no tie; a dogleg's sum of two lengths has no such guarantee, and `roundi` rounds a tie up. The cup is the tile at fault, and a dogleg's reason says so: "512 yd round the dogleg is a par 5, not par 4". Dogleg cases for the tests (`tests/test_validator.gd`): `ReactionHoles.dogleg_par4` (tee (6, 6), a 5-wide fairway east to (56, 6) then south to the cup (56, 50), round a pond) is 333 yd straight; its fairway 48 tiles out is (54, 4) to (54, 12), the turn (54, 8), 240.21 + 210.24 = 450 yd, still a par 4; east 270 yd to (60, 6) then south 270 yd to the cup (60, 60) is 382 yd straight (a par 4) and 240 + 271.66 = 512 yd round its corner, a par 5; east 210 yd to (48, 6) then south 210 yd to the cup (48, 48) is 297 yd straight (no rule 5 zone), its disc 237 yd out, the turn (48.08, 27.58), 339 yd, a par 4 and not a par 3; a hole with no fairway of its own beside, ahead of or behind another hole's fairway, and a 460 yd forced carry beside a parallel hole, keep the straight number. Cost (E3-S25 review, debug build, a loaded machine): a dogleg's line of play 0.58 ms (the disc read nearest its centre first, a one-tile ring read row by row, and a walk along its fairway toward the cup, the step toward the cup first), a straight hole's disc check 0.005 ms (its fairway on the line answers on the first tile; 0.03 ms when the disc was read as a box); `scripts/nine_holes_perf.gd` headless, `check_course` with nine straight holes 2.9 ms (2.65 at E3-S25, 2.42 before it, on a quieter machine), a click edit 0.45 ms (0.55 at E3-S25, 0.33 before). - Rule 5, landing zone: one disc for each hole of 300 yd or more (`zone_from_yd`, on rule 4's rounded line of play, D51, E3-S45; before D51 the straight line), whatever its par (D35, E6-S37); a hole under 300 yd has none. So every par 4 and par 5 (320 yd and up) has one, and so does a long par 3 of 300 to 319 yd, whose green a perfect drive cannot reach on level ground, so its tee shots come down short of it where another tee could stand. On a dogleg (D47's `turn_point`) its centre is the turn, where its tee shots come down, and a hole 300 yd or more round its corner has one even when its straight line is shorter (E3-S45: east 210 then south 210 yd, 297 straight, about 337 round); `CourseValidator.play_zone`. On a straight hole its centre is on the tee-to-cup line, `min(240 yd, yardage - 60 yd)` from the tee centre (`landing_zone`, which guests' sight check, D43, still reads on every hole): 240 yd on every hole that has one, as 300 - 60 is 240 (the clearance binds again only if `zone_from_yd` falls under 300 yd; with the old 250 yd par 4 and zone, 190 yd or more). - Footprint (an edit there closes an open hole, design doc "Undo"): every tile whose centre is within 30 yd of the line of play (D51, E3-S45: the tee-to-cup segment, or on a dogleg the two segments tee to turn and turn to cup, `CourseValidator.line_of_play`), a capsule that holds the landing zone. A surface or height edit on any footprint tile, or moving the hole's own tee or cup, closes the hole, even if it still passes. It stays closed until the player presses Open again, which reruns the validator. `CourseValidator.in_footprint` (and `near_line` for a line read once) is the one implementation, read by `course/builder.gd` and `guests/reaction_feed.gd`.
- Approach hint (E3-S39, never a rule, pillar 1): a tee whose centre is within `approach_hint_yd` 50 yd (`course/validator_tuning.tres`; guests' `play_clear_yd`, the landing area round an approach's rest) of another hole's cup stands where that hole's approach shots come down. `CourseValidator.hint_slots` gives both holes a hint on that tee (rule `HINT` 0, never in `check_hole`), `CourseBuilder.hints_at` keeps each slot's from the latest rerun, and the hole panel's details show the picked hole's in their own sand line under the rules ("hint at (92, 8): tee is 50 yd from hole 1's cup, where its approach shots land: guests waiting here may be hit"). The hole still opens. On nine_holes holes 1 to 2, 4 to 5 and 6 to 7 get one (tees 50 yd past the cups).
- Rerun after every edit: the footprint does not hold everything the rules read. Rule 3's path may leave it, and rule 5 reads other holes' tees. So after every build edit (a surface or height edit, or placing or moving any tee or cup) the validator also reruns for every open hole, and any open hole that now fails a rule closes, with that rule highlighted. ## Swing

- Three-click bar: start, power, accuracy. Bar fills in 1.2 s, runs to 110%. Kept at its E2-S7 numbers after the E2-S9 hand playtest (D24).
- Accuracy: each 10% off center = 2 degrees hook or slice. Outside zone = shank.
- The bar (E2-S7, `golf/swing.gd`, no scene; numbers in `golf/swing_tuning.tres`, tuned by hand in E2-S9). Click 1 starts the bar from 0; it reaches 100% in `fill_time` 1.2 s at bar speed 1.0 and runs on to `max_power` 110%, where it locks by itself. Click 2 locks the power at the bar, and the bar runs back at the same speed past the accuracy centre (0) to `bar_min` -30%. Click 3 locks the accuracy: the bar's position then, positive when early (above the centre), negative when late. Reaching `bar_min` with no third click is a late miss.
- Accuracy zone: `zone_half` 20% either side of the centre at 100% power or less (edges count as inside), so up to 4 degrees of curve. Early turns the heading right on screen (+heading, a slice for a right-hander), late turns it left (a hook), `deg_per_10pct` 2 degrees per 10% (D14: a heading offset at launch, the ball then flies straight). Overswing penalty: above 100% the zone narrows linearly to `overswing_zone_scale` 0.5 of its width at 110% (10% either side); the carry still grows to 1.1 times. Shank (outside the zone): the heading turns `shank_deg` 30 degrees toward the side of the miss, and the power is multiplied by `shank_power` 0.5. Every number is deterministic, so a swing's result is known at the third click.
- Bar speed (accessibility): a setting on the Play swing panel, `bar_speed_min` 0.5 to `bar_speed_max` 1.5 (default 1.0); the bar moves at `bar_speed / fill_time` per second, so 100% takes 2.4 s at the slowest and 0.8 s at the fastest. The accuracy return uses the same speed.
- Putter mapping (D49, E3-S51; linear from E2-S7 until then): the starting ground speed runs across the putter's range along `putt_bar_curve` 0.9 (`golf/clubs.tres`, `Club.putt_speed_at`), a blend of linear, `min + (max - min) × power`, and geometric, `min × (max / min)^power`: 0 is linear, 1 geometric. 0% is the slowest (1 yd/s) and 100% the fastest (18 yd/s), as before. Geometric gives every % of the bar the same share of the pace, so a short putt gets as much of the bar as a long one and a click error costs about the same share of the pace at any length. Shot model (D14). E2-S5 sets the numbers, E2-S7 builds the swing on it. Code (E2-S5): `golf/shot_model.gd` (`ShotModel.full`, `ShotModel.putt`) builds a `golf/shot_launch.gd` for a stroke, with no scene; `ShotLaunch.apply` sends a Ball off, and `ShotPrediction.run_launch` predicts it with the same step.

- Power is a share of carry, not of launch speed (design doc "Power"). A full club's target carry is `carry × power × lie`, where `lie` is the surface multiplier times the uphill multiplier. The launch speed is the one whose flat-ground carry at the club's launch angle equals that target. With no drag (Ball) that is `v_full × sqrt(power × lie)`, since flat carry is `v² sin(2θ) / g`. 110% overswing is 1.1 times the carry, before E2-S7's penalty.
- Surface lie multipliers scale carry, as the Distance column says, through that one product. Rough is one fixed value from 0.8 to 0.9 (start 0.85, a 15% loss), never rolled, so the same lie always gives the same marker. Bunker is 0.5, and the sand wedge is the only club there.
- Lie slope is measured at the rest point `p`, in steps, with heights from `HeightField.height_at`. Rise (uphill) looks ahead only: `(h(p + 5 yd along the aim) - h(p)) / 2 yd per step`. Ground behind the ball never counts, so flat ground ahead reads 0 wherever the ball rests. Tilt (sidehill) is centred across the aim, a 10 yd window: `(h(p + 5 yd to the left of the aim) - h(p + 5 yd to the right)) / 2 yd per step`, whose sign names the higher side. Ground on both sides counts, since a sidehill lie is ground falling across the stance, so a ball on flat ground beside a bank reads a tilt away from the bank. A rise above 0 multiplies carry by `1 - k × rise`, and a rise of 0 or below does nothing (lower ground ahead already lengthens the flight). A tilt turns the launch heading toward the lower side by `c` degrees per step of tilt. Left is as the player sees it on screen: the projection draws +y clockwise from +x, so left is the aim heading minus 90 degrees, and a positive tilt (left higher) adds to the heading. E2-S5 sets `k` and `c` against the lies targets. Lie slope and the surface multiplier apply to full clubs only, never to the putter. Why a window and not the gradient at `p`: off the green a ball rests only where the slope's pull is under the still-ball friction, a gradient under 0.14 (fairway, 0.36 steps per tile) or 0.25 (rough, 0.62), while a one-step rise between two tile centres is 0.4 across its whole band (only where a rise fades out at a corner is it gentler). So the gradient under a resting ball is almost always near 0, and a slope lie read from it would almost never happen. The window sees the rise just ahead of the ball, so it depends on how far short of a riser the ball rests. On flat ground facing a 2-step riser, the rise is 2 at the tile centre where the riser's band begins (a 2-step uphill lie), and `2 × (5 - d) / 5` at `d` yd short of it, 0 from 5 yd back. The lies targets name the spot for that reason. Rise and tilt count steps of height, not gradient, so `k` and `c` are per step whatever the window's length (hand calculation step 6).
- Hook, slice and sidehill curve are heading offsets at launch, added to the aim: 2 degrees per 10% off centre (Accuracy), plus the sidehill turn. The ball then flies straight. Ball gets no new term.
- A club's roll factor multiplies the ground speed the ball keeps after its first touchdown, after the surface's bounce friction. It is `Ball.roll_factor`, set at launch (E2-S5). It changes nothing else: the surface's Roll, bounce, scatter and still-ball friction stay as the surface table says. Scaling Roll instead would scale the still-ball friction too. On rough a roll factor under 0.68 would then make that friction (2.5 / factor yd/s²) beat the one-step pull (3.69 yd/s²), and balls would stop rolling down slopes (the GUT slope guard). On green it would move D7's hold. The putter has no roll factor: a putt starts rolling (`Ball.start_roll`).
- Putter: the bar sets the putt's starting ground speed across the putter's range (E2-S5 sets the range, E2-S7 the mapping: linear, Swing above), not a carry.
- Aim marker: for a full club, the first touchdown of a predicted perfect swing (100% power, accuracy at centre) with the selected club and lie, including rough, rise and tilt. For the putter (D21, E2-S20), the rest point of the putt, accuracy at the centre, at the power whose putt comes to rest nearest the cup (`PuttAim`, `golf/putt_aim.gd`); with no cup on the map, a putt at 100%. Both come from the same Ball step as the flight (E1-S7), so a perfect swing lands within 1 tile of the marker, and a putt struck at the shown power rests on it. The bar's locked power still decides the putt, so the marker shows the line and break of the putt the player needs, and the player still has to find its pace. The putter's power is solved: a safeguarded secant on the signed reach along the aim (how far past the cup's projection onto the aim the roll got before it stopped or turned back, negative short; E6-S12: the reach, not the rest, so it keeps growing with power on a bank a putt rolls back down; a cup square to the aim or behind it shows the 0% putt; E2-S20 review: measured along the ball-to-cup line, an aim turned 60 degrees off the cup showed a putt 0.6 yd further from it than the nearest the bar can make, and 3.4 yd at 90 degrees), from a first guess of distance / `full_putt_max_yd` (or the last solve's power while the ball stays put), stopping at a holed putt, a reach within `putt_aim_tolerance_yd` 0.1 yd that rested there, or `putt_aim_max_runs` 16 predicted putts. A roll that turns back level with the cup without dropping is aimed again to turn a `cup_radius` past it, so it drops on the way back. When the secant pins at the bar's edge (a cup out of reach, or a downhill putt too fast at 0%), finds a slope that is not positive, brackets a jump in the reach (a false-position bracket narrower than `putt_aim_refine_power`, where a putt just crests a riser), or a roll turns back at the cup twice, it scans `putt_aim_scan_powers` 5 powers from 0 to 100%. Where the scan shows a putt that stopped short without rolling back next to one that rolled back, it bisects for that edge (on a bank the nearest rest is often the hardest putt that still stops before it rolls back, at a jump the golden search cannot see past). Then it narrows a golden-section search on the distance to the cup around the nearest putt so far, down to `putt_aim_refine_power` 0.02 of the bar (E6-S12 and its review). Of every putt run it keeps the one resting nearest the cup, a dry rest before a drop. Centred pace (E3-S51, D49): a holed putt is only the first pace the solve found that drops, and from the flat-roll guess it often sat on the capture window's short edge (a 1 yd putt at 15.5% of the bar in a 12 to 38% window), so a click a little soft missed. So once a putt holes, while `putt_aim_max_runs` leaves room, the solve runs dry putts (no cup) on a secant aiming the roll to run `putt_aim_past_yd` 0.5 yd past the cup, within `putt_aim_past_tolerance_yd` 0.15 yd (`golf/swing_tuning.tres`), then strikes that pace with the cup and keeps it if it drops too; otherwise it keeps the first. The marker shows a holed putt either way; only the pace behind it moves to the window's middle (guests strike that pace too). Numbers in `golf/swing_tuning.tres`. Accuracy (E6-S12 and its review, `tests/test_shot_prediction.gd`): on banks rising two steps per tile, where no ball rests on the bank, the marker is within 1 yd of the nearest rest a brute-force sweep of the bar in 1% steps finds: over 210 cold solves (a bank rising along x, one along y, and a diagonal staircase whose risers cross the grid at 45 degrees; cups on, above and below the bank, uphill and downhill, aims up to 8 degrees off the cup) the worst was 0.56 yd further, where E2-S20's solve was up to 7.3 yd off, and 45 of 60 uphill solves more than 0.5 yd off. Other bank shapes (curved or mixed risers) are not measured. A capture window on the bank can be 1% of the bar wide; the turn past the cup finds it. Club legality (D13), checked in this order for each stroke:

1. On green: the putter only.
2. In a bunker: the sand wedge only.
3. Anywhere else: every club but the putter. The driver is allowed only on the hole's first stroke, from its tee marker, or from any tee tile. So a tee marker placed on fairway still allows the driver, and a ball that comes to rest on a tee tile later may use it too. The sand wedge is allowed on every lie except green.

Scoring (D12): stroke play. A hole's score counts strokes plus penalty strokes. The ball is picked up when that count reaches double par (6 on a par 3, 8 on a par 4, 10 on a par 5) without the ball holed, including when a penalty stroke reaches it. The score then records double par and a picked-up flag, and the card shows double par. A ball holed on the double-par stroke is holed, not picked up. E3-S3 guests use the same limit. Code (E2-S8): `golf/hole_score.gd` (`HoleScore`, no scene: `record_stroke(penalty, holed)`, `total`, `to_par`, `limit`, `picked_up`), with the multiple, `pickup_par_multiple` 2, in `golf/score_rules.tres`.

Playing the hole (E2-S8). Build and Play are one click apart (D2): the Play hole button (or Tab) puts the ball on hole 1's tee with a fresh score, and Back to Build (or Tab) returns with the course untouched (a test checks `CourseData.content_hash()`). Play reads the course and never writes it. The ball and the aim know hole 1's cup, so the live ball and the aim marker can hole out (D15). SwingControl counts each stroke it struck when the ball rests: strokes, the Ball's penalty strokes, holed. Play goes on until the ball is holed or picked up; then no swing starts, the aim and swing panel hide, and a picked-up ball is taken off the map. Entering Play again starts the hole over. ## Ball

- Parabola in 3D over height map, 60 Hz, deterministic by seed.
- One bounce on landing by surface, then roll down slope with surface friction.
- Water and OB: one-stroke penalty, drop at last entry point (Cup and hazards, D15).
- Code: `golf/ball.gd` (Ball, RefCounted, no scene) over `golf/height_field.gd`. Units are yards and seconds, x and y on the ground from the map corner, z up. Randomness comes only from the ball's own seeded generator.
- Height between tiles is bilinear between tile centres, so a one-step rise is a slope from one centre to the next. Past the map edge the edge height holds. The slope along an axis jumps at each tile centre on that axis; exactly on a centre it is the mean of the slopes on both sides (E2-S4), so a pit's floor line is level and a ball set on a rim's centre line feels half the wall.
- A flight that meets the ground (including a hillside) touches down at the contact point found inside the tick. That first contact is the landing position.

Starting values from E1-S6, in `golf/ball_params.tres`. Tune with `/balance`:

- Yards per height step: 2.0 (14 yards at height 7). Matches the 16 px riser on a 64x32 tile at 5 yards per tile.
- Gravity: 10.7 yd/s² (Earth). There is no drag yet, so carry comes from launch speed and angle alone. A 75 yd/s drive at 14 degrees carries about 247 yd and finishes about 296 yd on fairway (299 before E6-S10's fairway `drag_scale` 1.2).
- Rolling friction has two parts at roll factor 1.0: a constant 1.5 yd/s² (`roll_decel`) plus grass drag of 0.6 per second times the speed (`roll_drag`). A surface's friction is that divided by its Roll column, and roll 0 stops the ball where it touches down. The drag stops fast balls; the small constant lets slow balls feel the slope. Per surface (E1-S11), `drag_scale` multiplies the drag, and `rest_decel_scale` multiplies the constant for a still ball, rising linearly back to the full constant at `decel_ramp_speed`. Both are 1.0 except on green (see the table below), and `drag_scale` is 1.1 on cart path (E2-S4 review: with the lip no longer braking a ball, a driver down a cart path stepping 7 to 0 took 10.0 to 10.4 s on the ground; 1.1 brings every such drive under 10 s, the flat 100% drive from 9.2 s to 8.7 s and its roll-out past the carry from 182 to 175 yd). Per surface (E2-S4), `drag_full_speed` fades the drag in proportion to speed below it, so a slow ball running down a slope is not held to a crawl: 12 yd/s on rough, 0 (full drag at every speed) elsewhere. Without it a rough slope of one step per tile let a ball reach only 1.2 yd/s, and a still ball took 18.7 s to reach the floor of a 4-step rough valley. On green the still-ball constant is also the hold that keeps a slow ball on a gentle slope (D7, next lines), and ramping it back to full for moving balls keeps fast putts as they were. Fairway and tee `drag_scale` is 1.2 since E6-S10 (a 10 yd/s ball runs 8.7 yd on flat fairway instead of 9.8, still short of the green's 13.1; a test guards it). Downhill (E6-S10), per surface: `downhill_friction_scale` is the share of the rolling friction, constant part and drag alike, a ball keeps where the slope pulls it on along its line. The friction falls toward it in proportion to that pull along the ball's line over the constant friction (`Ball.downhill_share`), so in full on every slope of a step per tile or more off the green, and not at all on flat ground, straight across a slope or uphill: flat rolls and climbs are as before. 0.25 on fairway, tee and rough, 1.0 on cart path and green (Ball, Known limits, long slopes off the green).
- Slope pull along the ground is gravity × felt gradient / (1 + felt gradient²), at most gravity / 2, where the felt gradient is the terrain's gradient times the surface's `slope_scale`. The ground normal at touchdown tilts by the same felt gradient. The ball's height always follows the real terrain. A one-step slope pulls about 3.7 yd/s² at slope_scale 1.0 and 0.32 yd/s² on green (0.075, so a 40% step putts as a 3% break). Off the green the one-step pull must stay above the still-ball friction, `roll_decel` × `rest_decel_scale` / roll, on every rolling surface (a GUT test guards it), or balls never start down a slope. On green that friction is 0.48 yd/s² by design (D7, E1-S12): it holds a ball on a felt slope up to about 4.5%, so a one-step contour (3%, or 4.2% where two meet at a corner) holds it, while a green rising two steps per tile still rolls it down (a GUT test guards both sides). Steep green banks (E2-S4, D16, D22; retuned in E2-S11 and E6-S11): on each axis, the part of the terrain's slope above `steep_from` (0.4, one step per tile) is felt at `steep_scale` (20) on top of `slope_scale`, never more than the terrain's own slope. So every one-step contour, corners included (0.4 on each axis), putts exactly as before, while any axis steeper than about 1.05 steps per tile (0.42) is felt in full: two steps per tile as 80% instead of 6%, three as 120%. Just past a contour the felt slope already pulls a still ball well over the D7 hold (0.41 on one axis: 2.3 yd/s² against 0.48; 0.5 on one axis is felt in full, 4.3 yd/s²), so a ball does not creep where a slope is barely steeper than a contour (E2-S11: from E2-S4's `steep_from` 0.6 and `steep_scale` 1.5, the rim of a two-step green pit crept for up to 20.7 s). E6-S11 raised `steep_scale` from 3.0 to 20: at 3.0 a slope between 0.4 and about 0.46 pulled only a little over the hold, and since the hold rises back to the full constant friction by `decel_ramp_speed`, a ball there ran at a terminal speed of 0.3 to 0.6 yd/s for several seconds (random greens, Known limit below). Nothing else moved: the bank, pit, trench, gully and climb cases in Settle targets measure the same or less. A friction change alone cannot settle a steep green: at a felt 6% the pull is 0.64 yd/s², so even with no friction a still ball at the top of a two-steps-per-tile bank needs 6.7 s to reach its foot and over 2 s more to stop. Per axis, not by the slope's length, so a gentle gully floor between steep green walls stays a gentle break.
- Creases (E2-S4, per axis since its review): where the ground under a rolling ball turns up against it along an axis, at a tile centre (a valley or pit floor, the foot of an upslope), the ball loses that axis's part of its 3D velocity along the new ground's normal, as rigid ground held to the ball would take it. On that axis it keeps (1 + old slope × new slope) / (1 + new slope²) of its speed: 72% across a one-step V floor at full slope, all of it onto a flat foot. A crease never adds speed and never turns the ball back: past a 90 degree crease (walls steeper than 45 degrees, a pit or trench three or more steps deep) that axis stops, where reflecting it set up a 0.3 yd/s swing over the floor until the 60 s cap. At a convex crease (a lip, or onto a steeper downhill) nothing is lost, so a ball over a drop runs at least as far as on flat ground (a 20 yd/s fairway ball rests at x 45.6 to 46.5 over a 1- to 7-step drop against 43.9 on flat). The crease feels the felt slope only across a low point (falling, then rising, on that axis), so a steep green pit or gully floor damps the swing in full; elsewhere it feels `slope_scale` of the terrain's slope, so on green a steep bank pulls a ball back hard but does not take a putt's speed at its foot: a putt climbs a one-tile riser of 2 to 7 steps from 1 yd short at 9.4 to 10.3 yd/s and from 7.5 yd short at 13.1 to 14.0 yd/s (one step: 6.1 and 10.1; E2-S11, from 9.1 to 10.5 and 12.8 to 14.1 before). This is what damps a ball swinging across a valley floor; the friction alone took up to 12.9 s on cart path. Across a low point on an axis (falling, then rising: a valley or pit floor, not the foot of a bank or a lip), a ball also keeps only the surface's `floor_keep` of what that leaves it (E6-S10), as a ball thuds into the far wall: 0.4 on fairway, tee, rough and cart path, 1.0 on green. So across a one-step V floor off the green a slow ball keeps 29% instead of 72%, and a swing across the floor dies in a few passes. The thud fades with speed along that axis (E6-S10 review, `floor_keep_speed` 20 yd/s off the green, `Ball.floor_thud`): `floor_keep` for a still ball, rising in proportion to speed to all of it at 20 yd/s. - Rest speed 0.1 yd/s. - Minimum bounce speed 1.0 yd/s. Below it the ball rolls instead of bouncing. Safety cap: 3600 ticks (60 s).
- Bounce (E6-S34). At a touchdown the ground normal tilts by the felt gradient (Slope pull, above). The incoming velocity splits into its part along that normal (into the ground) and its part along the tilted ground. The part along the ground keeps 1 - bounce friction of itself (and, at the first touchdown, the club's `roll_factor`); the part into the ground comes back out along the normal times the surface's bounce; the scatter turns the ground part. If the part coming out is at least `min_bounce_speed` and the ball has not bounced yet, it flies with the sum, else it rolls with the ground part flattened. The bounce budget then caps the bounce's upward speed at gravity × `max_bounce_s` / 2, so over level ground it is back down within `max_bounce_s`: 1.1 s, in `golf/ball_params.tres` (0 turns the cap off). Why: on a one-step rise (a 0.4 bilinear slope, 21.8 degrees) the tilted ground points up the slope and the tilted normal leans back into the ball, so the ground part and the bounce both lift it, and a 110% driver went up at about 21 yd/s: 243 ticks and 100 yd in the air on fairway, 154 on rough, 426 on cart path (788 to rest), though the rise ends a tile on. 1.1 s is the smallest round value over the longest flat bounce on fairway, tee, rough and green (the PW at 110% onto flat fairway, 63 ticks, 1.05 s), so every flat-ground bounce there is unchanged bit for bit (a test); on cart path (bounce 0.5) the high flat bounces (up to 126 ticks) are cut, so a raw 100% drive onto flat cart path rests 142 yd past its carry, not 175, after 8.15 s on the ground (8.7 s before), and 8.3 s at 110% (8.9 s); every club onto flat cart path 8.2 s or less. Roll 0 takes trees out of the still-ball slope guard; `drag_full_speed` is 0.
- Scatter (`tree_scatter_min_yd` 5, `tree_scatter_max_yd` 10, `tree_scatter_cone_deg` 90, `tree_scatter_draws` 8): per draw, a distance then a turn, each `randf_range` from the ball's own generator. The reversed heading is the flight's ground velocity at the block, reversed.

- Cup: it sits at its tile's centre. It captures a ball on any surface, checked at the end of every rolling tick (a ball that rolls to rest inside the radius is captured too), never at touchdown. Touchdown speeds run far above any capture speed, so a check there would only add slam-dunks the speed rule rejects. A flight that touches down inside the radius bounces or rolls as usual and is checked on its next ground tick. E2-S6 sets the capture radius and the fastest speed that still captures.
- Water and OB entry point: the ball's ground track (its path in x and y, flight and roll alike) is walked back from where it stopped to the last point where it crossed from a tile outside the hazard into it. The hazard is water, or OB (an `oob` tile or off the map), whichever the ball stopped in. That crossing is the entry point.
- Drop point: the centre of the tile the track was over just before the entry point, if a ball may rest there. If not (water, `oob`, off the map or trees), keep walking back along the track to the first tile where it may. Never the cup's tile: the cup sits at its tile's centre, so a ball dropped there would be holed by any tap on the next stroke; the walk goes on past it as it does past trees. The same holds for a ball moved off trees to the nearest open tile centre (`Ball.may_place_on`). If there is none, it is the stroke's start. A start within `cup_radius` of the cup would leave a free tap-in, so then it is the nearest tile centre the game may place a ball on (`Ball.may_place_on`, the search a ball moved off trees uses); with none on the whole map (E2-S13), the ball goes on the cup's own tile `cup_clear_drop_yd` 2.4 yd from its centre, toward the start (`golf/ball_params.tres`; over `cup_radius`, under half a tile). So a drop never lands within `cup_radius` of the cup. The ball is placed there at rest and is not stepped again until the next stroke, so a drop on a slope cannot roll back into the hazard. One penalty stroke. The ball reports the penalty and the drop point (E2-S6) for the hole score (E2-S8).
- A ball that rolls into a tree tile stops at rest where it was on the tick before, outside the trees, as if against the trunks.
- Tree scatter (a flight blocked below E2-S6's block height): from the point where the flight was blocked, draw a distance of 1 to 2 tiles (5 to 10 yd) and a direction back toward the shot's start, within 90 degrees either way of the reversed heading, from the ball's own generator. A target on trees, water, `oob` or off the map, or within `cup_radius` of the cup (a still ball there would be holed on its next tick, E6-S13), is rejected and drawn again, up to 8 draws. Then it is the nearest tile centre a ball may rest on other than the cup's, searched ring by ring outward from the blocked point, ties in row-major order; with none on the whole map, the drop's last resort (the stroke's start, or `cup_clear_drop_yd` from the cup when the start is within `cup_radius`). A ball stopped on trees (only with `block_height` 0) moves the same way, so neither ever rests within `cup_radius` of the cup (tests in `tests/test_ball_surfaces.gd`). The ball comes down at the target with no speed and goes on as a still ball under the normal slope rules. It is deterministic by seed, so the prediction matches.

## Guests and reactions

- Daily arrivals scale with rating (E4-S5; until then the flat arrivals below). Guest: skill 1 to 10, patience 1 to 10, taste (Purist, Adventurer or Casual).
- Four checks per hole: fair, interesting, pace, challenge (score within 2 of par for skill).
- Fail 2 checks = complaint naming the hole. Pass 4 = praise. Otherwise a quiet neutral.
- Rating = rolling average of last 50 reactions, 1 to 5 stars. A reaction is one hole played (D39).
- Fee ceiling = $10 per star per round. Fee at ceiling cuts arrivals by one third.

What each of these measures (E3-S1, D38 to D43). Every number is in `data/guests.json` under the name given; `guests/` reads it from E3-S3 on and `/balance reactions` (E3-S8) tunes it. The same seed gives the same day: guests draw every random number from the day's seed and never read the clock. Loading the file checks its ranges (E3-S13, `GuestTuning._check_ranges`) and reports one error for each that does not hold, naming its keys (an assert in a debug build, `push_error` in any): each `*_sigma_best` at most its `*_sigma_worst`; `group_size_min` 1 or more and at most `group_size_max`; `skill_min` below `skill_max` (strictly, since the sigmas divide by the span); `patience_min` at most `patience_max`; each of the three `taste_weights` and `doubled_check_weight` above 0; the per-second rates `walk_yd_per_s`, `guest_ticks_per_s`, `sim_ticks_per_s` and `flight_draw_speed` above 0; the swing's shares (E3-S20): `swing_impact_share` 0 up to (not) 1, `swing_address_share` 0 to `swing_impact_share`; `guest_min_power` above 0 and at most 1; the day's clock: `day_s` and `stroke_s` above 0, `arrival_window_s` 0 to `day_s` (seconds of morning, so no group arrives after dusk; D46), `arrivals_per_day` 0 or more (E3-S13 review); plus the engine's older bounds (`sim_ticks_per_s` at least `guest_ticks_per_s`, `play_clear_yd`, `play_clear_share` and `cross_clear_yd` 0 or more, `cross_hold_max_s` at least 1, `cart_path_max_detour` at least 1, `max_steps_per_frame` and `frame_ticks_max` at least 1, `frame_guest_ms_max` and `sight_step_yd` above 0, `praise_passes` 1 to 4, `complaint_fails` at least 1, `guest_putt_max_runs` at least 2); and since E3-S36 the rest: the pace limits' `wait_base_s`, `wait_per_patience_s`, `walk_base_yd` and `walk_per_patience_yd` 0 or more (a negative would fail pace for every guest); `challenge_within` 0 or more and `casual_challenge_within` at least `challenge_within` (a Casual guest's band is the wider one, D42); `over_par_worst` 0 or more (no skill expects under par, D40); `eye_height_yd` above 0 (the eye stands above the tee, D43) and `mark_height_yd` 0 or more; `rating_window` at least 1. - Round length (D38, D46): about one simulated day for a nine-hole round, about 20 s a hole for a pair; the day (`day_s` 510 since D46, 180 before) is long enough that every group that tees off finishes its nine (Day length, below). - Club and aim (D41): a guest aims at the cup and picks the shortest legal club (D13) whose flat total on fairway (the Clubs table) times the lie reaches the cup, at power distance / (total × lie), at most 100%; when none reaches, the longest legal club at 100%. - Start $5,000. Until E4, it is the land budget and pays for parcels only (D19, D28; Land); E4-S1 folds it into the one balance. Upkeep $5 per open hole per day. Negative balance pauses building.
- Day = 8.5 real minutes at 1x, 2 minutes 50 s at 3x: 510 simulated seconds (`day_s` in `data/guests.json`), one guest sim step each (D38; D46 lengthened it from 180 s so every group finishes its nine, which makes the economy target's 25 to 35 days about 3.5 to 5.0 hours at 1x, 71 to 99 minutes at 3x, for E4-S8 to confirm or restate). Speeds: pause, 1x, 3x, skip to morning. Pause, 1x and 3x are buttons on the Play HUD's speed panel (keys P, 1, 3), with the day, its simulated time and the guests out (E3-S5); skip to morning waits for E4-S1's day-end accounting, and until then the next day starts at dusk.
