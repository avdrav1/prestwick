# E5 art pipeline: the options on paper (2026-11-10)

For E5-S1 ("Choose the art pipeline"). This prepares the `/decision`; it does not make it. The
choice is the user's.

## What the terrain art is now (D50)

E5-S1 was written when the ground was terraces: a surface diamond on top of stacked riser walls
(D5), so "one drawn surface and one riser" was the unit of terrain art. D50 (2026-11-06,
E3-S58) replaced that. Today the ground is a mesh over the bilinear surface the ball rolls on
(`course/terrain_mesh.gd`), and its art is:

1. **Surface textures**: the 9 flat 64 x 32 diamonds of `art/specs/surfaces.md` (one per
   surface, each with its colorblind pattern), mapped onto every tile's slope. A slope needs no
   frame of its own.
2. **The unowned-land hatch**: 16 frames of `art/specs/land.md`, mapped the same way.
3. **Slope shading, not art**: the slope light (`light_dir`, `light_strength`, `light_min`,
   `light_max` in `course/terrain_tuning.tres`), the height shade on land surfaces
   (`BuilderTuning.height_shade`), and the side faces at the map's front edges and water banks
   (`side_color`). These are numbers tuned against the final textures, not drawn.

There are no risers and no edge cue on the map (`art/specs/risers.md` and `edge_cue.md` are
marked not drawn; their sheets feed only `art/tiles/tile_preview.tscn`). So the unit of terrain
art is **one surface texture plus a retune of the slope light**, and the textures must:

- repeat on the tile lattice, (32, 16) and (32, -16) px, so neighbours join on any slope;
- keep the value measures of E6-S35 (`tests/test_course_view.gd`: Rec. 709 mean and fill, 5%
  `BAND_GAP` between rough's band over heights 0 to 7 and fairway, tee, green and trees; tee's
  fill 139 is the tight one);
- keep one pattern per surface (colorblind rule) that survives a grayscale screenshot at 0.5x.

The backlog's E5-S1 and E5-S2 are reworded to match (this commit).

## The sample: a procedural fairway in the game

Made in about 25 minutes: `scripts/art_sample.py` writes a copy of the Play surfaces sheet with
the fairway frame generated by a rule (mown stripes along one isometric axis, the light bands
sprinkled 30% with `tee_light`, the dark 14% with `fairway_dark`, a 4% grain, the plain
`fairway` still the most common colour; periodic in x + 2y and x - 2y mod 64, so it tiles).
`tests/fixtures/art_sample_shot.tscn` swaps it in (`ART_SAMPLE=1`) on the playtest nine.

- `docs/decisions/e5-s1-fairway-side-by-side.png`: hole 1 near the tee at 2x, cropped and
  scaled 2x, placeholder left, sample right.
- `docs/decisions/e5-s1-fairway-side-by-side-gray.png`: the same in Rec. 709 luminance.

What it shows:

- It reads as turf, not as a flat colour field, and the stripes are visible as faint diagonal
  bands. Its values do not move: Rec. 709 mean 151.9 against the placeholder's 152.0, fill 153
  (unchanged), so every E6-S35 band holds.
- Its grain makes the fairway look stippled, close to rough's stipple, so at 1x the two differ
  by value and stripe only. The colorblind rule names fairway "plain". A final procedural
  fairway should keep the stripes and drop most of the grain.
- The stripes are only about 14 px wide on screen: a pattern that repeats per tile cannot
  have a period over 64 px in x - 2y. SimGolf's broad mowing stripes (a tile or two wide) would
  need the mesh to sample a larger texture by world position (a 2 x 2 or 4 x 4 tile macro
  texture, a small change to `TerrainMesh`'s UVs). This applies to every option, not only this one.
- Effort: once the rule was written, retuning shares and colours took seconds per try, and
  the value measures could be checked before the game ran. That is the strength of the option.

## The options

Hours are one person's working time for the inventory above. Ranges are wide because nobody on
the project has yet drawn a final sheet. Do not trust any of them to better than plus or minus 40%.

### A. Hand-drawn pixel art in Aseprite, every sheet

- **Produces**: every sheet in the table, drawn by the team in Aseprite against the palette
  (Aseprite loads `art/palette.gpl`), exported by a CLI script to the spec's file names.
- **Hours**: about 71 h for a practised pixel artist; 110 to 140 h for a programmer learning
  as they go (the golfer's 96 frames dominate: 15 min a frame practised, 30 to 40 learning).
  Surface: about 1.3 h a texture, and it must be hand-tiled on the lattice.
- **Risks**: consistency is good when one person draws everything, poor if two do. Tiling
  textures by hand on a 2:1 lattice is fiddly. Grayscale bands are checked only after export
  (`tests/test_course_view.gd` catches a miss, but each fix is a redraw).
- **SimGolf**: closest to SimGolf's warmth if the artist is good; hand-drawn trees and
  clubhouse are where it shows most.
- **Pillar 4**: the most hours, and the time is hard to predict. Fits only if someone on the
  team already draws.

### B. Generated terrain base, Aseprite for sprites (recommended)

- **Produces**: surfaces and land hatch by rule in `scripts/` (as the sample: palette-only,
  lattice-periodic, value bands checked in Python and by `tests/test_course_view.gd`), with
  a hand touch-up pass in Aseprite on the 9 frames; trees, decor, clubhouse, golfer, ball,
  markers, HUD and icon drawn by hand. Markers, aim and swing bar stay in code.
- **Hours**: about 64 h practised (100 to 125 learning): surfaces 5 h instead of 12, the rest
  as A, plus 3 h of code if the mesh gets a macro-texture for broad stripes. The saving on
  the first pass is small; the gain is that every later palette or value change to the ground
  is a rerun in seconds, not a redraw, and the value bands are met by construction.
- **Risks**: generated texture can look like noise rather than a material (the sample's grain
  already leans toward rough). The touch-up pass and "fewer, larger marks" fix most of it.
  Two hands on the art (rules and pencil) is the consistency risk, kept small by both using
  the 32-colour palette and the same outline rules.
- **SimGolf**: SimGolf's ground is soft and painterly with mowing stripes; a rule gets the
  stripes and the softness (dithered blends) cheaply. The sprites carry the warmth.
- **Pillar 4**: best fit: the terrain, where the readability rules are strictest, becomes
  testable code; hand time goes where character matters. It is the backlog's own second
  option ("Aseprite with a generated terrain base").

### C. A licensed asset pack, adapted to the specs

- **Produces**: terrain, trees, a building and UI from one or two packs (CC0 or a paid licence
  allowing commercial use and modification), repaletted to `art/palette.gpl` and cut to the
  spec sizes; the golf-specific sheets (golfer swing, pin, tee blocks, ball) still drawn.
  Every file's source and licence recorded in `art/LICENSES.md`.
- **Hours**: about 45 to 60 h: 4 to finding and checking licences, 8 to repalette and cut
  terrain (and make it tile on the 64 x 32 lattice: most packs are not 2:1 at this size),
  4 the clubhouse, 3 trees and decor, 3 the UI, 12 to 20 the golfer (a generic 8-direction
  walker can be adapted; a golf swing in 8 directions almost certainly cannot), 8 for a
  consistency pass across sources.
- **Risks**: the art-spec skill says "do not pull images from the web", so this needs a
  `/decision` to amend that rule for licensed packs. Style mismatch between the pack and the
  hand-drawn golf sprites. Packs rarely match a 24 px figure or a 32-colour palette, and
  repaletting can wreck their shading and the grayscale bands. Licence terms must allow
  redistribution in builds strangers download (E5-S7).
- **SimGolf**: generic iso packs read as "any iso game", not as a golf course.
- **Pillar 4**: fewest hours only if a pack fits well; otherwise the adaptation eats the
  saving. Hard to know before the search is done.

### D. Commission an artist, with the specs as the brief

- **Produces**: every sheet in the table by one freelance pixel artist, work-for-hire with the
  rights assigned in writing; the team writes briefs, reviews and wires the art in.
- **Hours**: artist about 50 to 70 h (at an assumed USD 30 to 60 an hour, roughly USD 1,500 to
  4,000); team about 15 h (briefs from the specs, two review rounds, the grayscale checks).
  Calendar 3 to 6 weeks.
- **Risks**: availability and revision rounds set the date, not the team. The palette, the
  lattice tiling and the value bands are unusual constraints; the specs state them, but expect
  a round of fixes on the surfaces. Money.
- **SimGolf**: the best chance of SimGolf's warmth with one consistent hand.
- **Pillar 4**: fewest team hours and the most consistent result; fits if there is budget. A
  hybrid (B for terrain, D for the golfer and clubhouse only, about 30 artist hours) keeps most
  of the benefit at half the cost.

## Recommendation

**B: a generated terrain base with hand-drawn sprites in Aseprite**, markers kept in code.
The ground is where Prestwick's readability rules are strictest (tiling on a slope mesh,
patterns per surface, 5% value bands), and a rule meets those by construction and reruns when
the palette moves; the sample shows the values hold and the cost is minutes per surface. Hand
time goes to the golfer, clubhouse and trees, which carry the SimGolf warmth. If nobody on the
team draws, take B for the terrain and commission the golfer and clubhouse (D's hybrid).

Whichever is chosen, two follow-ups belong in the decision: whether the mesh samples a larger
macro-texture for SimGolf-width mowing stripes, and (for C) the art-spec skill's web-image rule.
