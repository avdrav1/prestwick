== where a skip's time went (headless, timing probes, eighteen-hole day, seed 1001, 933,469 Ball ticks) ==
before: total 3.02 s = serving the seconds' Ball work 2.17 s + the steps on the main thread 0.85 s
  Ball work: 1,029 batches of 6.2 guests (6,378 guest serves) over 600 seconds with work. The sum of each batch's busiest guest was 397,380 ticks against 265,612 for each second's busiest guest: a second ran in two or three batches one after another.
    Cause: _gather filled each batch's room under the sim's cap (sim_ticks_per_s 4,800 = ten guests' 480) with every guest on a hole, including those with no Ball work (a play READY to strike, waiting its turn, or FINISHED). Those spend 0 ticks, but each took 480 of the room, so guests with work were pushed into a later batch that waited for the first.
  Steps: 0.85 s, of which GuestWalk.route 0.52 s (1,720 walks, 0.30 ms each).
    Cause: route asks _path_route for the cart path tile nearest each end within cart_path_reach_yd (40 yd: a 19 x 19 square), read tile by tile through CourseData.in_bounds and get_surface: 0.21 ms an end. On a course whose path is not near a walk (the fixture has none) that was nearly the whole cost of a walk's route. On the playtest nine the steps went 0.82 -> 0.39 s with both fixes.
after: total 1.91 s = Ball work 1.49 s + steps 0.42 s
  Ball work: 513 batches of 5.1 guests (2,615 guest serves); the sum of each batch's busiest guest 237,856 ticks, at about each second's busiest guest (D63's floor).
  Steps: route 0.10 s (0.06 ms a walk). What is left in a step: the guests' strikes 0.09 s, hold_for 0.06 s, the reactions' statics 0.02 s, the rest the moves themselves.

== the fix ==
1. GuestBrain._gather passes over a guest whose play has no Ball work (GuestHole.State not PLANNING or FLYING), as serve already does. GuestHole.advance spends nothing in READY or FINISHED and nothing but a step changes that state, so every guest still gets what it would in turn: the same day bit for bit (skip_perf's PASS; tests/test_day_clock.gd compares every guest's state every 20 s of the eighteen-hole day's first 420 s, threaded and with serve_ahead, against one thread).
2. GuestWalk._nearest_path_tile reads the square round a walk's end from the cache's path grid (path_grid: every cart path and bridge tile, the same tiles _is_path reads), a row at a time (PackedByteArray.slice and find), in the same order, so ties go the same way; _path_route and why pass the grid. tests/test_guest_walk.gd compares it with the tile-by-tile read at 606 points each on a generated course with scattered path and bridges, by every edge and corner and off the map, and on a course with no path.
No target moved: 3 s a day holds at 18 holes, so no decision. The spare threads (24 logical cores, at most ten guests' budgets under the cap at once) were not needed; the step's moves (about 0.3 s a day) stay on the main thread.
